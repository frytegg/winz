# Copying website aspects: copyright, parasitism, trademarks, fonts, hackathon rules

> Code FD09 · slug policy-copying-and-ip · researched 2026-09-30 · verified 2026-09-30 · counts: verified 20 / corrected 11 / downgraded 0 / dropped 0 / added 1

## Summary

This is not legal advice. The author is not a lawyer, and nothing here was reviewed by one. The dossier maps the legal and ethical boundary around the owner's plan to "take the websites of the sponsors' customers, draw inspiration from them and copy certain aspects, using image to code", for a French resident presenting in Singapore, online and in Paris.

- **Ideas are free, expression is not.** The Cour de cassation says "les idées étant de libre parcours" (F10). The CJEU says interface components "differentiated only by their technical function" and subject matter "dictated by technical considerations, rules or other constraints" are not original (F03, F04). Functionality, languages and data formats are not protected either (F09). The US Copyright Office says layout and "look and feel" are not copyrightable (F08). /frontend may take conventions, information architecture, flows and measured facts. It may not take a site's specific arrangement, its text, images, icons, logos, fonts or code.
- **France adds two routes that need no originality.** One is parasitism: CA Paris, 16 Dec 2022, held that copying a site's sections in the same order, one identical text and near-identical product pages was parasitic "quand bien même chaque élément apparaît comme banal". It awarded EUR 20,000 in damages and EUR 8,000 in costs; the claimant had proved EUR 142,830.64 of graphic work and EUR 283,317.16 of IT work (F11). The other is EU design law: typefaces and graphic symbols have been listed as design "products" since the 2002 regulation, and since 1 May 2025 logos, graphical user interfaces and non-physical products are listed too and animation counts as part of a "design". An unregistered EU design protects them against copying for 3 years from first disclosure in the EU (F07). (corrected at verification: typefaces were not new in 2025.)
- **Text is the easiest thing to infringe.** An 11-word extract can be a reproduction if it carries the author's creativity (F05). Identical marketing text was decisive in the 2022 Paris case (F11).
- **A whole screenshot is not a quotation in France.** The Cour de cassation holds that the integral reproduction of a work "quel que soit son format" cannot be a "courte citation", and that showing a whole work on television, however briefly, is not one either (F32, added at verification). A full-page screenshot of a customer's product in the pitch or video cannot lean on the quotation exception.
- **Trademarks.** Naming a customer in plain text to explain where the protocol fits is lawful referential use, if it is necessary and honest. A logo, distinctive lettering, or anything that suggests a commercial connection is not (F12-F15). A disclaimer did not save the trader in Arsenal, and the Court only assumed, without deciding, that such a notice could ever be a defence (F14); this dossier treats a disclaimer as a required precaution, which is its own rule, not a holding. (corrected at verification: "a disclaimer is necessary" was presented as if sourced.) The French client-reference tolerance requires a contract in force, and a hackathon team has none (F16). Sponsors publish their own rules; BNB Chain lists "Powered by / Available on / Building on" and forbids "Official, Partnering, Collaborating" (F17).
- **Fonts.** Webfonts on customer sites are licensed to that customer's domain or organisation. Klim licenses them "for Your use only"; Grilli Type's web licence even excludes logos (F18). The open path is mechanical: classify the typeface, shortlist google/fonts families with the same tag and an OFL, Apache or UFL licence, and self-host them with the licence file (F19). Hotlinking Google Fonts cost a German site owner EUR 100 plus an injunction (F20).
- **Tools.** A widely used image-to-code tool reuses "the real logos/images from your screenshot" (F22). Figma pulled Make Designs after it produced mocks resembling Apple's Weather app (F23). awesome-design-md tells agents to build pages that "look like" DESIGN.md files named after real brands; its only notice disclaims ownership and warranty, not affiliation (F24).
- **Anthropic's own rule.** The Design type says "no other company's proprietary design", and its craft reference says Claude "must refuse" to recreate a company's distinctive UI unless the user works there (F25).
- **Hackathon rules.** TOKEN2049 Origins says "no code, designs, or prototypes before kickoff" (F27). ETHGlobal bans pre-existing project-specific designs or assets on Classic tracks, and its ETHOnline 2025 event page required AI attribution down to files and assets (F26). BNB Hack weights "Creativity and originality" at 25% and product quality and UX at 20%, and lets an existing product enter with a new integration shown before and after (F28). Devpost's own 2023 hackathon barred third-party trademarks from the visual portion of a submission without permission (F29). (corrected at verification: AI attribution is an event-page rule, not a general ETHGlobal rule; the Devpost text is one event's rules, not a template; BNB's existing-product route added.)
- **Policy.** Extract a written brief of ideas from customer sites and design original work from it. Name customers in words, never with their logos. Put a concept label on every frame that names a company. Keep a provenance log. Never ship image-to-code output made from a third party's screenshot. The full policy table and a disclaimer template follow the findings.

## Findings

### FD09-F01 — French copyright attaches without formality to any original work (software, graphic and typographic works included); unauthorised reproduction or adaptation "par un art ou un procédé quelconque" is unlawful and a délit de contrefaçon punishable by up to 3 years and EUR 300,000
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED (all five texts re-read in full on 2026-09-30; quotes verbatim; version dates 03/07/1992, 11/05/1994, 14/06/2009 and 05/06/2016 confirmed on Légifrance)
- **Claim:** The CPI protects every "oeuvre de l'esprit" whatever its genre, form, merit or purpose (L111-1 and L112-1, as restated by a Paris court). L112-2 lists graphic and typographic works (8°), works of applied art (10°) and software (13°). Under L122-4, any reproduction or representation, in whole or in part, made without consent is unlawful, and so is any "adaptation ou la transformation, l'arrangement ou la reproduction par un art ou un procédé quelconque". L335-3 makes such reproduction, and any violation of a software author's rights, a délit de contrefaçon. L335-2 punishes contrefaçon with 3 years' imprisonment and a EUR 300,000 fine.
- **Implication for /frontend:** An original customer page is a protected work, and rebuilding it by another process (an image-to-code conversion is a "procédé quelconque") is an adaptation that needs consent. By default, /frontend treats third-party UI, text, images and code as off-limits and takes only what is outside protection (F03, F04, F06, F08, F09).
- **Evidence:**
  - [Article L122-4 - Code de la propriété intellectuelle](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006278911) — Légifrance, version in force since 03/07/1992, accessed 2026-09-30 — statute.
    - "Toute représentation ou reproduction intégrale ou partielle faite sans le consentement de l'auteur ou de ses ayants droit ou ayants cause est illicite. Il en est de même pour la traduction, l'adaptation ou la transformation, l'arrangement ou la reproduction par un art ou un procédé quelconque."
  - [Article L112-2 - Code de la propriété intellectuelle](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006278875) — Légifrance, version in force since 11 May 1994, accessed 2026-09-30 — statute.
    - "Sont considérés notamment comme oeuvres de l'esprit au sens du présent code :" … "8° Les oeuvres graphiques et typographiques ;" … "10° Les oeuvres des arts appliqués ;" … "13° Les logiciels, y compris le matériel de conception préparatoire ;"
  - [Article L335-3 - Code de la propriété intellectuelle](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000020740345) — Légifrance, version in force since 14 June 2009, accessed 2026-09-30 — statute.
    - "Est également un délit de contrefaçon toute reproduction, représentation ou diffusion, par quelque moyen que ce soit, d'une oeuvre de l'esprit en violation des droits de l'auteur, tels qu'ils sont définis et réglementés par la loi. Est également un délit de contrefaçon la violation de l'un des droits de l'auteur d'un logiciel définis à l'article L. 122-6."
  - [Article L335-2 - Code de la propriété intellectuelle](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000032655082) — Légifrance, version in force since 5 June 2016, accessed 2026-09-30 — statute.
    - "La contrefaçon en France d'ouvrages publiés en France ou à l'étranger est punie de trois ans d'emprisonnement et de 300 000 euros d'amende." … "Lorsque les délits prévus par le présent article ont été commis en bande organisée, les peines sont portées à sept ans d'emprisonnement et à 750 000 euros d'amende."
  - [TGI Paris, 3e ch. 4e sect., jugement du 12 janvier 2017, Mycelium Roulement / Todo Material 3L](https://www.legalis.net/jurisprudences/tribunal-de-grande-instance-de-paris-3eme-ch-4eme-section-jugement-du-12-janvier-2017/) — Legalis (full text of the judgment), published 17 Jan 2017, accessed 2026-09-30 — court decision restating L111-1 and L112-1.
    - "Il se déduit de ces dispositions le principe de la protection d'une œuvre sans formalité et du seul fait de la création d'une forme originale. Le droit de l'article susmentionné est conféré, selon l'article L.112-1 du même code, à l'auteur de toute œuvre de l'esprit, quels qu'en soit le genre, la forme d'expression, le mérite ou la destination."
- **Limits / false positives:** Only original forms are protected (see F03, F04, F06); a page assembled from conventions may not be. The criminal maxima are rarely what happens in practice, and civil claims are the usual route. French law governs where protection is claimed for France (F31).

### FD09-F02 — French copyright exceptions are narrow: a private copy must be strictly private and not for collective use, and a quotation must be short and justified by the critical, polemical, educational, scientific or informational nature of the work it sits in
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
- **Claim:** L122-5 lets an author's disclosed work be copied only within listed exceptions. They include copies "strictement réservées à l'usage privé du copiste et non destinées à une utilisation collective" (2°) and "analyses et courtes citations" justified by the character of the work in which they are incorporated (3° a), "Sous réserve que soient indiqués clairement le nom de l'auteur et la source". The private-copy exception expressly excludes copies of software other than the backup copy. The exceptions "ne peuvent porter atteinte à l'exploitation normale de l'oeuvre ni causer un préjudice injustifié aux intérêts légitimes de l'auteur".
- **Implication for /frontend:** A mockup that reproduces a customer page is not a quotation inside an analysis, so no exception covers it. A reference board of customer screenshots shared across a team is arguably collective use, not the private copy of one person; this is my inference and it is untested. A whole-page screenshot of a customer's product does not become a "courte citation" by being small: the Cour de cassation rejects the reduced-format argument for integral reproductions (F32). If the pitch must show the customer's current flow, show a cropped detail of the one element under discussion, name the author and source, and keep it clearly not our UI; better, redraw the flow as a neutral diagram in the protocol's own design. (corrected: the original implication said a small, attributed full screenshot "fits the quotation purpose better"; French case law in F32 contradicts that for integral reproductions. Claim extended with the verified attribution condition, the software carve-out and the three-step clause.)
- **Evidence:**
  - [Article L122-5 - Code de la propriété intellectuelle](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006278917) — Légifrance, page shows "en vigueur depuis le 01/01/2023", accessed 2026-09-30 — statute.
    - "Lorsque l'oeuvre a été divulguée, l'auteur ne peut interdire :" … "Les copies ou reproductions réalisées à partir d'une source licite et strictement réservées à l'usage privé du copiste et non destinées à une utilisation collective" … "Les analyses et courtes citations justifiées par le caractère critique, polémique, pédagogique, scientifique ou d'information de l'oeuvre à laquelle elles sont incorporées"
    - Verification re-read (full text, 2026-09-30): "3° Sous réserve que soient indiqués clairement le nom de l'auteur et la source :" … "à l'exception des copies des oeuvres d'art destinées à être utilisées pour des fins identiques à celles pour lesquelles l'oeuvre originale a été créée et des copies d'un logiciel autres que la copie de sauvegarde" … "Les exceptions énumérées par le présent article ne peuvent porter atteinte à l'exploitation normale de l'oeuvre ni causer un préjudice injustifié aux intérêts légitimes de l'auteur." The page shows "Version en vigueur depuis le 01 janvier 2023", modified by loi n° 2021-1104 of 22 August 2021, art. 32.
- **Limits / false positives:** No French decision on a screenshot of a web page as such was found; the integral-reproduction rule comes from cases on paintings and murals (F32; a photograph case is reported only in commentary). The version date is confirmed (1 January 2023). EU harmonisation of the quotation exception (Directive 2001/29, Art. 5(3)(d)) was not researched and could soften the French "intégrale" rule.

### FD09-F03 — CJEU, BSA (C-393/09): a graphical user interface is not protected as a computer program, but can be protected as a work if it is its author's own intellectual creation; components differentiated only by their technical function are not original, because idea and expression merge
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED (paras 46, 48, 49 verbatim; Third Chamber, 22 December 2010, ECLI:EU:C:2010:816)
- **Claim:** The Court (Third Chamber, 22 December 2010) held that a GUI is outside the Software Directive but can be a "work" under Directive 2001/29. National courts must look at "the specific arrangement or configuration of all the components" to find what is original. Components whose expression is dictated by their technical function do not qualify.
- **Implication for /frontend:** A customer's interface can be protected as an arrangement, so /frontend must not reproduce it. Conventional components whose form follows function (a nav bar, an input with a label, a primary button, a data table) are free building blocks. The skill should borrow conventions and never a site's specific configuration.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Judgment of 22 December 2010, Bezpečnostní softwarová asociace, C-393/09](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62009CJ0393) — CJEU (Third Chamber), ECLI:EU:C:2010:816, accessed 2026-09-30 — judgment.
    - "46 Consequently, the graphic user interface can, as a work, be protected by copyright if it is its author's own intellectual creation."
    - "48 When making that assessment, the national court must take account, inter alia, of the specific arrangement or configuration of all the components which form part of the graphic user interface in order to determine which meet the criterion of originality. In that regard, that criterion cannot be met by components of the graphic user interface which are differentiated only by their technical function."
    - "49 … where the expression of those components is dictated by their technical function, the criterion of originality is not met, since the different methods of implementing an idea are so limited that the idea and the expression become indissociable."
- **Limits / false positives:** The originality test is left to national courts; the ruling gives no website-specific examples. Verification note: the same judgment holds that "television broadcasting of a graphic user interface does not constitute communication to the public of a work protected by copyright" because viewers receive it "solely in a passive manner, without the possibility of intervening" (paras 57-58). That concerns the right of communication to the public, not reproduction, and it sits in tension with the French rule on integral representation in F32 (see Contradictions 10).

### FD09-F04 — CJEU, Cofemel (C-683/17): a work must reflect the author's free and creative choices and be identifiable with precision; subject matter dictated by technical considerations, rules or constraints is not original, and an aesthetic effect alone does not make something a work
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED (paras 30, 31, 55 verbatim; Third Chamber, 12 September 2019)
- **Claim:** The concept of "work" is autonomous in EU law and has two cumulative conditions. The first is originality, meaning the subject matter reflects the author's personality through free and creative choices. The second is identification with sufficient precision and objectivity. A design's "specific, aesthetically significant visual effect" is not enough to make it a work.
- **Implication for /frontend:** A customer page built from framework defaults and web conventions may be weakly protected, and a distinctive landing page full of deliberate choices is protected. The same test applies to /frontend's own output: only its free creative choices make it protectable and distinctive, which lines up with the anti-slop goal.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Judgment of 12 September 2019, Cofemel, C-683/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0683) — CJEU (Third Chamber), accessed 2026-09-30 — judgment.
    - "30 … if a subject matter is to be capable of being regarded as original, it is both necessary and sufficient that the subject matter reflects the personality of its author, as an expression of his free and creative choices"
    - "31 On the other hand, when the realisation of a subject matter has been dictated by technical considerations, rules or other constraints, which have left no room for creative freedom, that subject matter cannot be regarded as possessing the originality required for it to constitute a work"
    - "55 It follows that the circumstance that designs such as the clothing designs at issue in the main proceedings generate, over and above their practical purpose, a specific and aesthetically significant visual effect is not such as to justify those designs being classified as 'works'"
- **Limits / false positives:** The case concerned clothing designs; applying it to web pages is by analogy. It does not settle how much creative freedom a given landing page had.

### FD09-F05 — CJEU, Infopaq (C-5/08): an extract of 11 consecutive words can be a "reproduction in part" if it contains an element that expresses the author's own intellectual creation
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED (paras 46-48 verbatim; Fourth Chamber, 16 July 2009, ECLI:EU:C:2009:465; para 50 adds that multiple extracts can cumulatively reconstitute "lengthy fragments", which supports a checker that counts overlaps across the whole site, not per sentence)
- **Claim:** Parts of a work are protected when they carry the originality of the whole. Isolated sentences or parts of sentences can do so. Storing and printing 11-word extracts was capable of being reproduction in part.
- **Implication for /frontend:** Never reuse customer or sponsor headlines, taglines or microcopy. A checker can flag long runs of words shared between the built site and scraped customer copy. Any threshold (for example 8 or 11 consecutive words) is a heuristic inspired by this case, not a legal rule.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Judgment of 16 July 2009, Infopaq International, C-5/08](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62008CJ0005) — CJEU (Fourth Chamber), ECLI:EU:C:2009:465, accessed 2026-09-30 — judgment.
    - "47 … the possibility may not be ruled out that certain isolated sentences, or even certain parts of sentences in the text in question, may be suitable for conveying to the reader the originality of a publication such as a newspaper article"
    - "48 … the reproduction of an extract of a protected work which, like those at issue in the main proceedings, comprises 11 consecutive words thereof, is such as to constitute reproduction in part within the meaning of Article 2 of Directive 2001/29, if that extract contains an element of the work which, as such, expresses the author's own intellectual creation; it is for the national court to make this determination."
- **Limits / false positives:** The 11 words are the facts of the case, not a floor or a ceiling. Generic phrases ("Get started", "Connect wallet") are not creative, and single words are not protected (para 46).

### FD09-F06 — A Paris court required the claimant to explain its website's originality, then held that a conventional e-commerce layout, tree structure and red/black/grey palette were not protected and that copying them was not parasitic (TGI Paris, 12 January 2017)
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED (all four quotes verbatim on Legalis, page dated mardi 17 janvier 2017; the court also held that general terms of sale "ne sont susceptibles d'aucune appropriation dans la mesure où leur contenu se trouve formulé à l'identique dans de nombreux sites", see Contradictions 3)
- **Claim:** When originality is contested, the claimant must explain it. Rubric order and site structure reflected "savoir-faire commercial" and a utilitarian need, not creative effort. Red and black was a banal pairing. The resemblances (search, login and contact placement, tabbed product modules, a column grid) were common to merchant sites, so the unfair-competition and parasitism claim also failed.
- **Implication for /frontend:** Industry conventions are the legitimate "take" side of the policy: search placement, account and cart position, product grids, tabbed modules, common colour pairings. This is not permission to copy a distinctive, invested-in combination (F11).
- **Evidence:**
  - [TGI Paris, 3e ch. 4e sect., 12 janvier 2017, Mycelium Roulement / Todo Material 3L](https://www.legalis.net/jurisprudences/tribunal-de-grande-instance-de-paris-3eme-ch-4eme-section-jugement-du-12-janvier-2017/) — Legalis, published 17 Jan 2017, accessed 2026-09-30 — first-instance judgment (full text).
    - "Lorsque l'originalité de l'œuvre est contestée en défense, cette originalité doit être explicitée par celui qui s'en prétend auteur, seul ce dernier étant à même d'identifier les éléments traduisant sa personnalité."
    - "Elle ne justifie pas des choix qui ont présidé à l'ordonnancement des rubriques et à l'arborescence du site, à la mise en perspective des produits présentés , qui attestent plus d'un savoir-faire commercial commun à d'autre sites marchands qu'à un réel effort créatif, dés lors qu'ils permettent de naviguer aisément sur le site et répondent à un impératif utilitaire largement répandu pour le commerce en ligne."
    - "l'utilisation des couleurs rouge et noir en raison du contraste créé par l'association de couleurs opposées est banale."
    - "les éléments de ressemblances visuelles évoquées par la demanderesse tiennent à la reprise d'éléments qu'on trouve habituellement dans les sites marchands tels que l'emplacement des fonctions recherche, connexion et contact du service"
- **Limits / false positives:** This is a first-instance, fact-specific ruling, and the claimant pleaded originality weakly. The defendant had also changed its site after a formal notice.

### FD09-F07 — EU design law expressly covers typefaces and graphic symbols (since 2002) and, since 1 May 2025, logos, graphical user interfaces, non-physical products and animations; an unregistered EU design lasts 3 years from first disclosure in the EU and is enforceable against copying, subject to limits for private non-commercial, experimental, citation or teaching, referential, and comment, critique or parody uses
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
- **Claim:** Regulation (EU) 2024/2822 redefined "design" to include "movement, transition or any other sort of animation". It redefined "product" to include items in "a non-physical form", listing "graphic works or symbols, logos, surface patterns, typographic typefaces, and graphical user interfaces". Typefaces were not new: the original 2002 definition already listed "graphic symbols and typographic typefaces" (6/2002, Art. 3(b)); the EUR-Lex summary names graphic works or symbols, logos, surface patterns and GUIs as what the product definition "now covers". Protection requires novelty and individual character. The holder of an unregistered design can act "only if the contested use results from copying", and independent creation by a designer unfamiliar with the design is not copying. Article 20 limits the right for private non-commercial acts, experiments, citation or teaching (with the source named), acts "identifying or referring to a product as that of the design right holder", and comment, critique or parody. The last three apply only if "compatible with fair trade practices". Unregistered protection lasts 3 years from first disclosure in the Union (Regulation 6/2002, Art. 11(1)). Articles 3 (definitions, amending point 9) and 19-20 (rights and limitations, amending point 17) are not among the points deferred to 1 July 2026, so they have applied since 1 May 2025 (Art. 3 of 2024/2822). The texts were codified as Regulation (EU) 2026/715 of 11 March 2026. (corrected: the heading and claim implied typefaces were first covered in 2025; they were listed in 2002. Application-date article now read through a reprint.)
- **Implication for /frontend:** A customer site first disclosed in the EU in the last three years may be an unregistered EU design even where its copyright originality is doubtful, and its motion and transitions count. Once /frontend has studied the site, it cannot rely on the independent-creation defence. The output must therefore produce a different "overall impression" (Art. 10 of 6/2002, the scope test), not a near-copy with swapped colours.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Regulation (EU) 2024/2822 of 23 October 2024 amending Regulation (EC) No 6/2002](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2822/oj/eng) — European Parliament and Council, OJ 18.11.2024, accessed 2026-09-30 — regulation.
    - "“design” means the appearance of the whole or a part of a product resulting from the features … including the movement, transition or any other sort of animation of those features"
    - "“product” means any industrial or handicraft item, other than a computer program, regardless of whether it is embodied in a physical object or materialises in a non-physical form, including: … (b) graphic works or symbols, logos, surface patterns, typographic typefaces, and graphical user interfaces"
    - "4. The holder of an unregistered EU design shall be entitled to prevent acts referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 only if the contested use results from copying the protected design. The contested use referred to in the first subparagraph shall not be deemed to result from copying the unregistered EU design if it results from an independent work of creation by a designer who may be reasonably thought not to be familiar with the design made available to the public by the holder."
    - Art. 20(1): "(a) acts carried out privately and for non-commercial purposes; (b) acts carried out for experimental purposes; (c) acts of reproduction for the purpose of making citations or of teaching; (d) acts carried out for the purpose of identifying or referring to a product as that of the design right holder; (e) acts carried out for the purpose of comment, critique or parody" … Art. 20(2): "Paragraph 1, points (c), (d) and (e), shall only apply where the acts are compatible with fair trade practices and do not unduly prejudice the normal exploitation of the design, and in the case referred to in point (c), where mention is made of the source of the product"
  - [Council Regulation (EC) No 6/2002 on Community designs](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32002R0006) — Council, 12 December 2001, accessed 2026-09-30 — regulation (original text).
    - "Article 11 … 1. A design which meets the requirements under Section 1 shall be protected by an unregistered Community design for a period of three years as from the date on which the design was first made available to the public within the Community."
    - "Article 10 … 1. The scope of the protection conferred by a Community design shall include any design which does not produce on the informed user a different overall impression."
    - Verification re-read, Art. 3(b) (original text): "“product” means any industrial or handicraft item, including inter alia parts intended to be assembled into a complex product, packaging, get-up, graphic symbols and typographic typefaces, but excluding computer programs;"
    - Verification re-read, Art. 11(2): "a design shall be deemed to have been made available to the public within the Community if it has been published, exhibited, used in trade or otherwise disclosed in such a way that, in the normal course of business, these events could reasonably have become known to the circles specialised in the sector concerned, operating within the Community."
  - [European Union designs (summary)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/european-union-designs.html) — EUR-Lex summaries, last update 7.4.2025, full page re-read at verification 2026-09-30 — official summary.
    - "Regulation (EU) 2024/2822 has applied since 1 May 2025, although some of its articles requiring the adoption of implementing or delegated acts will apply from 1 July 2026."
    - "The definition now covers sets of articles and spatial arrangements of items intended to form an interior or exterior environment, along with graphic works or symbols, logos, surface patterns and graphical user interfaces."
  - [Regulation (EU) 2024/2822, Article 3 (reprint)](https://hodak.nl/legislation/en/verordening-2024-2822-modellen/artikel-3/) — Hodak.nl legislation reprint, accessed 2026-09-30 (added at verification; a reprint, not the Official Journal) — regulation text.

### FD09-F32 — Cour de cassation: the integral reproduction of a work, whatever its format, is not a "courte citation", and showing a whole work on television, whatever its form and duration, is not one either (added at verification)
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** added at verification
- **Claim:** The Assemblée plénière (5 November 1993, 92-10.673, Bulletin 1993 A.P. n° 15 p. 28) quashed a ruling that a reduced-format reproduction of three whole artworks in an auction catalogue was a short quotation: "la reproduction intégrale d'une oeuvre, quel que soit son format, ne peut s'analyser comme une courte citation". The first civil chamber (4 July 1995, 92-20.199, Bulletin 1995 I n° 296 p. 207) applied the same rule to a TV programme that showed murals in full: "la représentation intégrale d'une oeuvre, quelles que soient sa forme et sa durée ne peut s'analyser comme une courte citation". A 2005 decision (1re civ., 25 January 2005, 02-10.370), seen only in a search-result excerpt of its Juricaf page, repeats that "la reproduction intégrale d'une oeuvre ne peut s'analyser en une courte citation" and lists the 1995 decision among concordant precedents.
- **Implication for /frontend:** A full-page screenshot of a customer's product, however small, in the pitch deck, the README or the demo video, is not covered by the French quotation exception if the page (or an image or illustration on it) is a protected work. Showing it for two seconds in a video does not help. Policy row 20 is tightened accordingly: prefer a neutral redrawn diagram of the flow in the protocol's own design; if the real product must appear, show only a cropped detail of the element being criticised, with the author and source named (L122-5 3°), and record the decision in the provenance log.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Cour de cassation, Assemblée plénière, 5 novembre 1993, 92-10.673](https://juricaf.org/arret/FRANCE-COURDECASSATION-19931105-9210673) — Cour de cassation, via Juricaf (AHJUCAF case-law database; reprint, not the Légifrance copy), accessed 2026-09-30 — judgment.
    - "Vu les articles L. 122-4 et L. 122-5.3°.a du Code de la propriété intellectuelle (l'article 41.3° de la loi du 11 mars 1957) ; Attendu que la reproduction intégrale d'une oeuvre, quel que soit son format, ne peut s'analyser comme une courte citation ;"
    - "Attendu que, pour rejeter cette demande, l'arrêt retient que la réduction substantielle du format des oeuvres permet d'en ravaler la reproduction au rang de simple allusion aux oeuvres d'origine et de l'assimiler à de courtes citations s'incorporant au catalogue, oeuvre à caractère d'information ; Qu'en statuant ainsi, la cour d'appel a violé les textes susvisés ;"
  - [Cour de cassation, 1re chambre civile, 4 juillet 1995, 92-20.199](https://juricaf.org/arret/FRANCE-COURDECASSATION-19950704-9220199) — Cour de cassation, via Juricaf (reprint; the page has encoding damage, text read through it), accessed 2026-09-30 — judgment.

- **Limits / false positives:** The cases read concern paintings and murals, not web pages (law-firm commentary reports a 7 November 2006 decision applying the rule to a photograph; not read); a page assembled from conventions may not be a work at all (F06), in which case the question does not arise, but its photos and illustrations usually are. ## Template disclaimer for a concept mockup naming a real company

Where it goes: on the mockup frame itself, as a visible label, not only in a footer. It also appears as on-screen text in every video shot where the mockup is visible, and in a "Third-party names" section of the README. The reason is Arsenal (F14): a notice that stays behind when the image travels does not protect.

What it does not do: it does not license a copy of anyone's design, text or images (F01, F07). It does not cure a presentation that already suggests a commercial link (F13, F14). It works only on top of rows 7 and 9 of the policy table.

> Concept illustration. [Company] is named only to show where [Protocol] could be used. [Company] has not reviewed, endorsed or sponsored this project, and the [Protocol] team has no relationship with [Company]. [Company] and its logos are trademarks of their owner. The interface shown is our own design and does not reproduce [Company]'s product. All figures are fictional.

## Contradictions between sources

1. **Typefaces.** US regulations exclude "Typeface as typeface" from copyright (37 CFR 202.1(e), F08). The French code lists "oeuvres graphiques et typographiques" among protected works (L112-2 8°, F01), and EU design law lists "typographic typefaces" as design products (F07). A typeface can be free to imitate in the US and protected in France or the EU. The font files are separately bound by their EULA (F18).
2. **CSS.** The US Copyright Office calls CSS a method of formatting and "generally will refuse to register claims based solely on CSS" (F08). EU and French law protect program expression in any form (F09) but give no answer on whether a stylesheet is a program. Law-firm blogs asserting that "source code" is protected do not address CSS specifically (see "could not be found").
3. **Website look-and-feel in France.** TGI Paris 2017 rejected both copyright and parasitism for a copied e-commerce layout (F06). CA Paris 2022 found parasitism for a copied e-commerce site (F11). The facts differ rather than the law: the 2022 case had identical text, systematic reproduction, a proven EUR 426k investment and a site singular in its market. The practical lesson is that outcomes turn on accumulation and on text. Added at verification: the two courts also treated identical text differently. The 2017 court held that general terms of sale "ne sont susceptibles d'aucune appropriation" because the same wording appears on many sites, while the 2022 court counted an identical presentation text against the copier. Boilerplate that the whole market uses is free; a site's own marketing copy is not.
4. **Anthropic's Design type contradicts itself.** SKILL.md says "No AI tropes (gradient washes, left-border cards, emoji; Inter, Roboto, Arial)". craft.md, for work not governed by a brand, recommends "gradient meshes, noise textures, geometric patterns, layered transparencies, dramatic shadows, decorative borders, grain overlays", and its slop list adds Fraunces. The owner's tells #1 and #20 side with SKILL.md (F25).
5. **Design type: match a named brand versus refuse to recreate one.** SKILL.md says that with "a brand or app the user names, match exact colors, type, spacing, radii and fonts". craft.md says Claude "must refuse" to recreate a company's distinctive UI unless the user works there. They are reconcilable only if "match" applies to the user's own brand (F25).
6. **BNB Chain brand guidelines.** "Projects can add the BNB Chain logo to their website … only when talking about something related to building on" versus "Projects should seek approval from BNB Chain before using the logo" (F17).
7. **TOKEN2049 page versus the owner's brief.** The official page promises "Demo on the TOKEN2049 stage to 25,000 attendees" and dates 6-8 Oct. The owner's brief says 6-7 Oct and "recorded video only, no live stage demo". The builderbase event page narrows the promise to "Top 5 demo live on the TOKEN2049 Stage", which fits a recorded-video judging round followed by a finalist stage demo (added at verification). The researcher reported that two fetches of the same page disagreed on the application deadline (14 Sep versus 18 Sep) and the prize pool ("To be announced" versus $150,000). A raw curl at verification returned only "18 Sep Application Deadline" and "$150,000 PRIZE POOL", with no "14 Sep" or "To be announced" anywhere in the HTML; the other variant could not be reproduced and may have come from the summarising fetch or a cached copy (F27). (corrected at verification.)
8. **Logo versus word in references.** APIE observes that client-reference practice "semble être de reproduire le logo" (F16). New Kids limits nominative use to what is reasonably necessary and credits the absence of the logo (F15). The contexts differ (a real contract versus no relationship), and the owner's case falls on the no-relationship side.
9. **Is a hackathon demo "commercial"?** Singapore exempts use "for a non-commercial purpose" (F12). The EU design limitation covers acts "privately and for non-commercial purposes" (F07). Arsenal defines course of trade as "commercial activity with a view to economic advantage and not as a private matter" (F14). No source settles whether a prize and investor-facing demo is commercial, and the answer may differ by country. For parasitism in France the question matters less: Com. 16 February 2022 (F10) says the action lies "quels que soient le statut juridique ou l'activité des parties" and left liability standing against a non-profit association and a foundation, although the 2024 definition still speaks of "un opérateur économique" (and cites the 2022 decision for it).
10. **Showing a third-party interface in a video (added at verification).** The CJEU held that television broadcasting of a graphic user interface is not a communication to the public of it, because viewers cannot interact with it (BSA paras 57-58, F03). The Cour de cassation held that showing a whole work on television, whatever its form and duration, is not a short quotation (1995, F32). The rulings answer different questions (whether a GUI's interactive character is communicated, versus whether an exception covers the display of a whole artwork), so neither makes a recorded demo that shows a customer's full page safe: the page's images, illustrations and logo are ordinary works and marks shown in full.
