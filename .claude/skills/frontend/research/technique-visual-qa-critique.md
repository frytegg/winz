# Automated visual QA and model critique of a built UI
> Code FD17 · slug technique-visual-qa-critique · researched 2026-09-30 · verified 2026-09-30 · counts: verified 12 / corrected 14 / downgraded 1 / dropped 0 / added 2

## Summary

- Off-the-shelf multimodal judges are unreliable on fine design distinctions. On UIClip's held-out UI pairs (designer-rated BetterApp plus synthetic JitterWeb) GPT-4V's overall accuracy was 51.58% (and it refused about 10% of items) while a task-trained CLIP model (UIClip) reached 75.12% (F01). On graphic design, no pre-trained open-weight VLM judge exceeded 0.55 agreement with a five-designer majority, against a 0.741 single-designer ceiling (F16). MLLM pairwise accuracy is near chance when two UIs differ slightly and reaches about 90 to 93% only when humans see a large difference (F06).
- The best measured web-page judges: WebDevJudge (ICLR 2026) found a plateau near 70% pairwise agreement against 84.56% human-human agreement (F07); ArtifactsBench reached up to 90.95% with per-task checklists, several staged screenshots and the source code, on model-versus-model comparisons (F08).
- Prompting that measurably helps: pairwise instead of absolute scores (+8 points), binary checklist items instead of Likert scales, code plus screenshots instead of screenshots alone, screenshots at all and then several of them (Gemini-2.5-Pro: none 79.06%, one 87.10%, several 90.95%), multi-dimensional scoring (69.5% vs 48.5%), few-shot expert critiques retrieved by visual and task similarity (+55% critique quality, still 0.48 vs 0.75 for designers) (F02, F07, F08, F09). Asking twice with the order swapped is MT-Bench's conservative rule (F14), but the one study that measured it found no agreement gain (F28); it stays in the acceptance rule as a guard against false accepts, not as an accuracy lever. (corrected at verification: 79.06% was the no-screenshot condition, not one screenshot; retrieval used visual and task similarity; the order swap was not a measured gain)
- What does not work: letting the model localize problems (best bounding-box IoU 0.222, GPT-5 below 0.20), reasoning modes for aesthetics, free-form "give feedback" prompts without guidelines, Nielsen's interaction heuristics applied to static screenshots (F03, F05, F16, F19).
- Iteration: gains concentrate in the first round (Claude 4.5 Sonnet +4.9%, then +0.9 and +0.3 points, at about 20 times the tokens), keeping the best-scoring version gave +10.8% against +6.1% after cycle 3 (the best is picked by the same judge that scores it, so part of that gain is selection on judge noise), a 7B base model's revisions regressed after 2 to 3 rounds without an acceptance rule, and LLM feedback becomes less accurate once the design has improved (52% to 39% "accurate") (F05, F09, F10, F11, F13). (corrected at verification: "doubles" replaced by the measured figures and the selection caveat) Policy: at most 3 rounds, accept a candidate only if it strictly improves under a deterministic gate plus an order-swapped pairwise verdict, otherwise revert to the best-so-far.
- Deterministic QA must carry what judges miss and what designers actually complained about on Claude Opus 5 landing pages (legibility on 10 of 10 briefs, broken layouts in 29 write-ups, pages too long in 32; F17): WCAG contrast with pixel sampling where axe returns "incomplete" for gradients and alpha (F24), clipping via scrollWidth, reflow at 320 CSS px, 24 px targets, CLS 0.1, fonts actually rendered via CDP, broken images, HTTP 4xx which Playwright's `requestfailed` does not report (F23, F25, F26).
- Tools: Playwright plus @axe-core/playwright cover the gates; Lost Pixel is archived, Galen's last release is from 2019, AIM needs Docker plus MongoDB; re-implement AIM's grid-quality counts (2 px tolerance) in the DOM script (F20, F27).
- Thresholds with an external source exist for contrast, target size, reflow, CLS, body size, line length and line height (F22, F23, F26). None exist for "number of font sizes", spacing-scale adherence or colour counts; on-scale spacing is a Tailwind default, and the only measurement found (anonymous, three AI outputs, grade C) says spacing rhythm does not separate AI from human sites, so those checks must be calibrated on the owner's corpus and a human-designed control corpus (F21).
- Human checkpoints where evidence says automation fails: choosing a direction among clearly different options, choosing the logo, and the final sign-off before recording (F06, F16, F18). Everything else can be automated with the gates and the critic protocol in Deliverables A and B below.

## Findings

### FD17-F01 — Off-the-shelf multimodal LLMs pick the better UI of a pair near chance; a model trained on design pairs reached 75%
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
- **Claim:** In UIClip's evaluation on held-out pairs from BetterApp (real app screens rated by 12 designers) and JitterWeb (synthetic pairs, an original page against a deliberately degraded copy), general large vision-language models (GPT-4V, LLaVA) performed around random guessing at picking the better UI of a pair (GPT-4V 51.58% overall across both sets, refusing about 10% of items; LLaVA 54.59%). The UIClip variant trained with web pairs reached 75.12% average overall accuracy. Designers themselves agreed weakly (Krippendorff's alpha 0.37 on 10 shared pairs; the paper attributes it to the task's subjectivity). (corrected: the 51.58% is the overall figure across the designer-rated and the synthetic pairs, not designer-rated pairs alone; alpha 0.37 is weak, not moderate, agreement)
- **Implication for /frontend:** Do not let a single zero-shot "rate this design" call decide whether an iteration is better. Use the judge for coarse, checklist-grounded, pairwise decisions, and let deterministic measurements carry fine distinctions. The designer alpha of 0.37 is also a ceiling reminder: even humans disagree on fine taste, so the target is the owner's taste, set at human checkpoints.
- **Evidence:**
  - [UIClip: A Data-driven Model for Assessing User Interface Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12500) (HTML v1: https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12500v1) — Jason Wu, Yi-Hao Peng, Amanda Li, Amanda Swearngin, Jeffrey P. Bigham, Jeffrey Nichols; arXiv v1 18 Apr 2024; accessed 2026-09-30 — peer-reviewed paper (UIST 2024, per the model card below).
    - "Despite their much larger size, all LVLMs performed very poorly on design quality assessment, often around the level of random guessing. Interestingly, GPT-4V (overall 51.58%) refused to provide a response for around 10% of examples"
    - "For both BetterApp and JitterWeb, the UIClip variant trained with web pairs performed the best, with an average overall accuracy of 75.12%."
    - "We used this initial set to compute Krippendorff's alpha score, with α = 0.37."
  - [biglab/uiclip_jitteredwebsites-2-224-paraphrased_webpairs_humanpairs](https://huggingface.co/biglab/uiclip_jitteredwebsites-2-224-paraphrased_webpairs_humanpairs) — BigLab (CMU/Apple authors), model card, lastModified 2024-10-22, accessed 2026-09-30 — official model card.
    - Command: `curl -sL https://huggingface.co/api/models/biglab/uiclip_jitteredwebsites-2-224-paraphrased_webpairs_humanpairs` → `lastModified 2024-10-22T05:07:40.000Z`, license `mit`, files `config.json, model.safetensors`; README: "This is a model described in the publication "UIClip: A Data-driven Model for Assessing User Interface Design" presented at UIST 2024"
- **Limits / false positives:** GPT-4V is a 2023 model; newer frontier models were not tested in UIClip. UIClip itself is built on CLIP B/32 with a fixed 224×224 input: at inference the screenshot's shorter side is resized to 224 px and a 224 px window slides along the longer side ("The input screenshot is first resized so that its smaller dimension is equal to 224 pixels"), so a 1440×900 desktop capture is judged at roughly 358×224. Its training data is mostly synthetic "jittered" web pages (JitterWeb, 2.3 million examples) plus a small human-rated set, and the weights (model.safetensors 605,156,676 bytes, plus a PyTorch runtime) are heavy for a machine with 4.8 GB free; at most an optional tie-breaker. (corrected: UIClip uses a sliding 224 px window over the resized screenshot rather than shrinking the whole page to one thumbnail)

### FD17-F02 — Few-shot expert critiques retrieved by similarity plus visual prompting raised LLM critique quality by 55%, still well below designers
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
- **Claim:** UICrit collected 3,059 critiques on 983 mobile UIs from seven experienced designers (of the 3,059, 2,283 came from the designers, 256 were Gemini comments the designers validated, 520 from both). With Gemini Pro Vision, 8-shot examples retrieved by joint visual and task similarity plus coordinate-based visual prompting raised the average critique quality score (which includes bounding-box accuracy) from 0.31 (zero-shot) to 0.48 (+0.17, p = 5e-4, "55 percent"), in a study where 6 design experts rated critiques for 6 UIs. Human designer critiques scored 0.75. Zero-shot produced more comments (29 vs 14) with more invalid ones, which the authors say "could be caused by Gemini's hallucinations". Raters agreed only fairly (Fleiss' kappa 0.29 to 0.31 across the paper's three rating studies). (corrected: kappa range is 0.29 to 0.31, not 0.29 to 0.30; hallucination is the authors' hedged explanation, not an attribution; the score includes box accuracy; the study size is added)
- **Implication for /frontend:** Seed the critic with a small bank of expert-style critiques and retrieve the most visually similar ones as few-shot examples (the owner's past frontends with their tells, and human control sites). Cap the number of issues per round: a zero-shot critic produces more, and worse, comments.
- **Evidence:**
  - [UICrit: Enhancing Automated Design Evaluation with a UI Critique Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08850) (HTML v3: https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08850v3) — Peitong Duan, Chin-yi Chen, Gang Li, Bjoern Hartmann, Yang Li; arXiv v3 13 Aug 2024; ACM UIST 2024; accessed 2026-09-30 — peer-reviewed paper.
    - "However, current LLM-based techniques do not yet match the performance of human evaluators."
    - "our setup outperformed the zero-shot baseline by 0.17 (p=5e−4) in average quality score, which corresponds to a 55 percent increase."
    - Table 7: "Zero-shot 0.31 29 2.6 / 8-shot, Visual and Task Similarity, Coordinates Visual Prompting 0.48 14 2.1 / Human 0.75 17 1.3" (avg comment quality, number of comments, avg rank)
    - "we computed the Fleiss Kappa inter-rater reliability score ... for the critique ratings across participants and obtained a value of 0.29, which implies fair agreement."
    - "zero-shot has considerably more comments than the other two conditions, which could be caused by Gemini's hallucinations"
  - [google-research-datasets/uicrit](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/uicrit) — Google Research; `gh api repos/google-research-datasets/uicrit` → `pushed=2024-11-19T19:53:09Z | archived=true`; accessed 2026-09-30 — dataset repository, now archived ("collected for our UIST '24 paper").
    - README: "Note that this version of the dataset is around 3 times larger than the one discussed in the paper, where each UI screen was evaluated by a single human annotater. This larger version of the dataset contains 11,344 design critiques."
- **Limits / false positives:** Mobile screens only (RICO), one model (Gemini Pro Vision, 2024), six UI screens in the few-shot comparison and six in the validation study, small rater pools. The dataset has no web landing pages, so the few-shot bank for /frontend has to be built from the owner's own corpus. The public repository is archived and its CSV (about 1,000 UIs, three annotators each, 11,344 critiques) is not the dataset the paper's numbers were measured on. (corrected: repository archived status and the dataset-version mismatch added)

### FD17-F03 — Models cannot localize UI problems precisely; measure geometry from the DOM and let the model cite numbered marks
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
- **Claim:** Bounding boxes produced by an MLLM for its own critiques overlap the true region poorly: in UICrit the average IoU was 0.004 with the plain screenshot, 1.1e-5 with a grid, 0.222 with numbered patches and 0.186 with coordinates. In AesEval-Bench (ICLR 2026) the best model on bounding-box localization of aesthetic issues, GPT-5, scored below 0.20 average IoU; in a GPT-4o ablation, removing the JSON layout metadata hurt precise localization more than the other tasks, which the authors "hypothesize" is because "metadata provides explicit layout information, which aids bbox prediction". Anthropic's vision documentation states that Claude's coordinate and localization outputs are approximate and counting is approximate. Set-of-Mark prompting (overlaying alphanumeric marks on segmented regions) is the established way to make a model refer to regions. (corrected: the metadata effect is one ablation on GPT-4o plus the authors' hypothesis, not an established finding)
- **Implication for /frontend:** Never ask the critic for pixel coordinates or measurements. Overlay DOM-derived numbered marks (element IDs mapped to selectors) on the screenshots, pass the element metadata (rects, computed styles) alongside, and require every critique to cite a mark ID. Spacing, alignment and size numbers come from the DOM script, not from the model.
- **Evidence:**
  - [UICrit](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08850v3) — as in F02; accessed 2026-09-30.
    - Table 5: "Visual Prompt Method Avg. Bounding Box IoU Screenshot Only 0.004 Screenshot with Grid 1.1e-5 Screenshot with Patches 0.222 Screenshot with Coordinates 0.186"
  - [Can Vision Language Models Assess Graphic Design Aesthetics? A Benchmark, Evaluation, and Dataset Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01083) (HTML v1) — Arctanx An et al.; arXiv 1 Mar 2026; "ICLR 2026" in the arXiv comments; accessed 2026-09-30 — peer-reviewed paper.
    - "For the bbox prediction problem, even the best-performing model, GPT-5, scores below 0.20, highlighting the substantial difficulty of precisely localizing aesthetic issues."
    - "Finally, metadata has the greatest effect on precise localization, where its absence causes a larger decline in performance compared to aesthetic judgment or region selection. We hypothesize that this is because metadata provides explicit layout information, which aids bbox prediction in localization tasks."
  - [Vision (Claude API docs)](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision) — Anthropic; fetched as markdown 2026-09-30 — official docs.
    - "Spatial reasoning: Claude's coordinate and localization outputs are approximate."
    - "Counting: Claude can give approximate counts of objects in an image but might not always be precisely accurate, especially with large numbers of small objects."
  - [Set-of-Mark Prompting Unleashes Extraordinary Visual Grounding in GPT-4V](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11441) — Jianwei Yang, Hao Zhang, Feng Li, Xueyan Zou, Chunyuan Li, Jianfeng Gao; arXiv v2 6 Nov 2023; accessed 2026-09-30 — preprint.
    - "overlay these regions with a set of marks e.g., alphanumerics, masks, boxes. Using the marked image as input, GPT-4V can answer the questions that require visual grounding."
- **Limits / false positives:** No study measured DOM-mark prompting on web landing pages specifically; the benefit of marks is inferred from Set-of-Mark and from UICrit's patch result. AesEval-Bench's items are Crello graphic designs with injected perturbations (moved elements, swapped fonts, altered colours), not web pages.

### FD17-F04 — What the critic can see: images are cut into 28 px patches and downscaled above 2576 px (high-resolution tier), with a stricter limit above 20 images
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED
- **Claim:** Claude processes images as 28×28 px patches. Claude 4.7 and later models accept up to a 2576 px long edge and 4,784 visual tokens; other models 1568 px. Larger images are downscaled, which can make text less legible. Requests with more than 20 images face a stricter per-image limit (keep each side at or below 2000 px). Images under 200 px risk hallucination.
- **Implication for /frontend:** Feed the critic viewport-sized tiles, not tall full-page captures: a 1440×9000 full page would be shrunk to about 412 px wide and the body text would disappear. Desktop 1440×900 and mobile 390×844 at DPR 2 (780×1688) both fit the high-resolution tier. Keep one critic call at 20 images or fewer. (The Playwright side of this, including full-page captures that never trigger scroll reveals, is covered in FD06-F10 and FD06-F13.)
- **Evidence:**
  - [Vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision) — Anthropic; accessed 2026-09-30 — official docs. Command: `curl -sL https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision.md`; lines 1236 to 1310 read:
    - "Each patch is a 28×28-pixel block of the image, referred to as a visual token."
    - "| High-resolution | Claude 4.7 and later models | 2576 px | 4784 |" and "| Standard | All other models | 1568 px | 1568 |"
    - "If a single API request contains more than 20 images, a stricter per-image dimension limit applies ... resize each image so that neither dimension exceeds 2000 px, or keep the request to 20 or fewer image and document blocks."
    - "Take into account that your image might be resized if it is too large ...; this might, for example, make text less legible."
    - "Claude might hallucinate or make mistakes when interpreting low-quality, rotated, or very small images under 200 pixels."
- **Limits / false positives:** These are API rules. How Claude Code's Read tool resizes a screenshot before the model sees it was not verified in this session.

### FD17-F05 — GPT-4 heuristic evaluation had lower precision than a single designer, and its feedback got worse after the design improved
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED
- **Claim:** On 12 UIs with 100 ground-truth violations, GPT-4 had precision 0.603 vs 0.829 for an average human expert, recall 0.380 vs 0.336, F1 0.466 vs 0.478. In an iterative study with 12 designers, suggestions on the initial UIs were 52% "Accurate" and 47% helpful; in later rounds, after designers improved the UIs, 39% were accurate and 33% helpful ("unsuitable as an iterative tool"). Without the guideline text in the prompt, suggestion quality was worse. Expert raters agreed poorly on the suggestions (0.155 accuracy, 0.085 helpfulness).
- **Implication for /frontend:** Expect the critic to keep producing issues after the page is good, with a rising share of wrong ones. Stop conditions must come from gates and pairwise acceptance, not from "the critic has nothing left to say". Always pass the rubric/guideline text in the prompt.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Generating Automatic Feedback on UI Mockups with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13139) (HTML v1) — Peitong Duan, Jeremy Warner, Yang Li, Bjoern Hartmann; arXiv comment "To appear at ACM CHI 2024"; accessed 2026-09-30 — peer-reviewed paper.
    - Table 2: "Precision 0.603 0.829 Recall 0.380 0.336 F1 0.466 0.478" (GPT-4, human evaluator average)
    - "We found that GPT-4 was generally accurate and helpful in identifying issues in poor UI designs, but its performance became worse after iterations of edits that improved the design, making it unsuitable as an iterative tool."
    - "52 percent were rated Accurate, 28 percent Partially Accurate, and 20 percent Not Accurate; 47 percent were considered helpful or very helpful ... However, score distributions were lower for later rounds ... 39 percent were rated Accurate, 26 percent Partially Accurate, and 35 percent Not Accurate; 33 percent were considered helpful or very helpful"
    - "We conclude that while the LLM could give plausible UI feedback without passing in heuristics, the quality of the suggestions is worse."
    - "The average inter-rater reliability score, based on the first round of suggestions, is 0.155 for accuracy and 0.085 for helpfulness"
- **Limits / false positives:** Text-only GPT-4 reading a Figma JSON, not screenshots; 2023 model; mobile mockups. The direction of the effect (worse feedback on better designs) is the transferable part.

### FD17-F06 — MLLM pairwise UI judgments are near chance for small differences and 90 to 93% for large ones; exact Likert matches are about 37 to 38%
- **Grade:** B · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
- **Claim:** Against 15,000 responses from 500 MTurk participants on 30 UIs and nine factors, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o predicted pairwise human preferences with about 60% overall accuracy (59.98% and 59.60%); accuracy started near 50% for small human score differences and rose to about 90% (GPT-4o) and 93% (Claude) for large differences. The paper's Table 3 (all factors) gives exact Likert accuracy of 38% (Claude 3.5 Sonnet), 37% (GPT-4o) and 35% (Llama-3.2-11B), and within-one-point accuracy of 77%, 72% and 74%; its results text agrees ("ranges from 35% to 38%"; "from 72% to 77%"), while its discussion states "Claude 48% and GPT 40%" and its introduction says all three exceed 75% within one point, so the paper is internally inconsistent and the table values are used here. Models underestimated "Interesting" (human 5.82, Claude 5.10, GPT-4o 4.73). Each judgment was sampled 10 times and the median taken. (corrected: the 48%/40% exact-match and ">75% within one point" figures come from the paper's discussion and introduction and contradict its own Table 3; the title's range changed accordingly)
- **Implication for /frontend:** A pairwise verdict is only informative when the two candidates differ clearly. For small tweaks (a spacing value, a shade), use deterministic metrics; for direction choices between similar options, ask the human. Sample the judge several times and aggregate.
- **Evidence:**
  - [MLLM as a UI Judge: Benchmarking Multimodal LLMs for Predicting Human Perception of User Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08783) (HTML v1) — Reuben A. Luera et al. (UC Berkeley, Adobe Research, Georgia Institute of Technology); arXiv 9 Oct 2025; accessed 2026-09-30 — preprint with data.
    - "the same models perform less effectively in terms of exact accuracy (Claude 48% and GPT 40%) and when there is a small degree of difference in pairwise tasks. In fact, the models performed near random on pairwise tasks with small degrees of difference." (discussion section; contradicted by the results below)
    - Section 4.1.2: "The average accuracy of MLLMs over all factors ranges from 35% to 38%, with Claude having the highest accuracy of 38%. The average ±1-accuracy is significantly higher, from 72% to 77%."
    - Table 3, row "All": "Claude-3.5 0.37 0.51 0.38 0.77 ... GPT-4o 0.41 0.52 0.37 0.72 ... Llama-3.2 0.48 0.54 0.35 0.74" (MSE, MAE, accuracy, ±1-accuracy)
    - "GPT-4o and Claude have the largest increases, starting near 50% and ending close to 90% and 93%, respectively."
    - Table 4 overall: "Claude-3.5 ... 59.98 GPT-4o ... 59.60"
    - "we evaluate each UI and factor by each MLLM 10 times and then take the median score"
- **Limits / false positives:** Crowdworker ground truth (MTurk), not designers or hackathon judges; 30 UIs from "a professional UI dataset" whose footnote is reallygoodemails.com, an email-design gallery (24 professionally made, 6 made from scratch, some deliberately degraded "to maintain a diverse dataset of both high and low quality designs"), not landing pages; 2024-generation models; not peer-reviewed as far as checked; the paper reports conflicting numbers for the same metric (see Claim).

### FD17-F07 — Best web-app judges plateau near 70% against 84.56% human agreement; pairwise beats single grading by 8 points; code outweighs screenshots
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED
- **Claim:** WebDevJudge (ICLR 2026) compared LLM, MLLM and agentic judges with expert preferences on paired web implementations. The best judge (GPT-4.1, pairwise) agreed 70.34%, Claude-4-Sonnet 70.18%, while human pairwise agreement was 84.56%; larger models plateau near 70%. Pairwise improved agreement by over 8 points over single-answer grading, and inter-model consistency was above 75% for pairwise vs 50 to 65% for single grading. In single grading, binary rubric items beat Likert scales. Code was the dominant modality: for Claude-4-Sonnet pairwise, image only 59.48, code only 67.58, both 70.18. Position bias persisted despite instructions (GPT-4.1 preferred the second position in 15.8% of cases). Query-grounded rubrics raised human inter-annotator agreement with ties from 65.0% to 92.0%. Agentic evaluator workflows did not beat plain models ("compounding errors").
- **Implication for /frontend:** The critic compares incumbent vs candidate, never scores in isolation; it receives the component source plus screenshots; its rubric is derived from the spec as binary items (one per feature and per property). Do not build an elaborate agentic judging pipeline for aesthetics.
- **Evidence:**
  - [WebDevJudge: Evaluating (M)LLMs as Critiques for Web Development Quality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18560) (HTML v3, 3 Mar 2026) — Chunyang Li, Yilun Zheng, Xinting Huang, Tianqing Fang, Jiahao Xu, Lihui Chen, Yangqiu Song, Han Hu; ICLR 2026 (listed on [ML Anthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2026/li2026iclr-webdevjudge/)); accessed 2026-09-30 — peer-reviewed paper.
    - "The top-performing evaluator, GPT-4.1 under the pairwise paradigm, attains an agreement rate of 70.34%." ... "consistently plateauing around the 70% agreement rate."
    - "Across the board, the pairwise paradigm yields an average improvement of over 8.0% in agreement rate compared to single-answer grading."
    - "in single answer grading, the binary Rubric approach substantially outperforms the multi-point Likert scale"
    - Table 4: "Claude-4-sonnet 55.66 (↓ 3.51) 61.77 (↑ 2.60) 59.17 59.48 (↓ 10.7) 67.58 (↓ 2.60) 70.18" (single: image only, code only, both; pairwise: image only, code only, both)
    - "Human Pairwise comparison - 84.23 - 83.11 - 86.47 - 84.56"
    - "Despite explicit instructions to ignore order and remain objective, models still exhibit a systematic preference for responses in a specific position"
    - Table 1: "w/ tie 65.0 53.0 92.0 90.0" (inter-annotator without rubric, annotator vs original labels, with human-written rubric, with LLM-generated rubric)
    - "Agentic workflow suffers from compounding errors."
- **Limits / false positives:** The annotation guidelines weight function over looks ("In general, the completeness of functionality takes precedence over aesthetics, unless the user query explicitly requests a focus on design."), so these numbers bound judging of working web apps, not of visual taste alone.

### FD17-F08 — A checklist-guided judge with staged screenshots and code reached up to 90.95% pairwise agreement with front-end engineers
- **Grade:** B · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED
- **Claim:** ArtifactsBench renders each artifact, captures three staged screenshots, and gives the judge the task, the full answer (code) and a per-task 10-dimension checklist. In a double-blind study on 280 queries and 6 models judged by front-end engineers, Gemini-2.5-Pro's pairwise agreement rose from 79.06% (no image) to 87.10% (one screenshot) to 90.95% (several screenshots); Qwen2.5-VL-72B went 61.80% to 71.34%. Captions instead of images were inferior; removing the answer (code) degraded agreement. Model rankings matched WebDev Arena at 94.4% normalized Footrule consistency.
- **Implication for /frontend:** Give the critic several states per feature (initial, mid-interaction, result), the relevant source, and a feature-specific checklist. This is the configuration with the highest measured agreement.
- **Evidence:**
  - [ArtifactsBench: Bridging the Visual-Interactive Gap in LLM Code Generation Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04952) (HTML v2, 29 Sep 2025) — Chenchen Zhang, Yuhang Li et al. (Tencent Hunyuan); accessed 2026-09-30 — preprint with data.
    - "Our automated evaluation achieves 94.4% ranking consistency with WebDev Arena ... and up to 90.95% pairwise agreement with human experts."
    - "We run a double-blind study on 280 queries and 6 models, judged by multiple front-end engineers. Agreement is measured by Pair ACC. Incorporating execution screenshots markedly improves agreement, and multiple screenshots better capture dynamics (Table 4). Replacing images with captions helps but remains inferior when visual capability is strong; removing the answer degrades quality, confirming the need for code-aware judging"
    - Table 4: "Gemini-2.5-pro 79.06% ... Gemini-2.5-pro 87.10% ... Gemini-2.5-pro 90.95%" (w/o img, w/ img, w/ imgs)
- **Limits / false positives:** Pairs are outputs of different models on the same task, so quality gaps are often large (see F06). The judge is also scoring code quality. See the contradiction with WebDevJudge's 70% ceiling and with ReLook's description of this study.

### FD17-F09 — A visual critic loop gave most of its mean gain in cycle 1; keeping the best-scoring version gave 10.8% against 6.1%; code quality slipped only for the weakest generator
- **Grade:** B · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
- **Claim:** On 600 WebDev Arena tasks, with Claude 4.5 Sonnet as the fixed visual critic, a generate-critique-improve loop raised Claude 4.5 Sonnet's mean judged score from 7.584 to 7.958 (+4.9%) in cycle 1, 8.021 (+5.8%) in cycle 2 and 8.048 (+6.1%) in cycle 3, while cumulative tokens rose from 6,446 to 131,550. Taking the best-scoring version across cycles gave +10.8%, and the best version came after at least one cycle for 86% of tasks; the "best" is the version the same VLM judge scored highest. Refinement without a critic gave +1.5% (Distill-Qwen-14B). ### FD17-F28 — Asking twice with the order swapped did not raise agreement with experts in the one study that measured it; it is a conservative guard, not an accuracy lever (added at verification)
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** MEASURED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED (added at verification)
- **Claim:** WebDevJudge (ICLR 2026, the same paper as F07) tested MT-Bench's conservative rule (F14) in its Appendix E.1: each pair judged twice with positions swapped, a preference kept only if both orders agree, otherwise a tie. Agreement with the expert labels did not improve and mostly fell slightly: Claude-4-Sonnet 70.0 to 68.6 (direct) and 70.2 to 68.3 (Likert); GPT-4.1 68.7 to 68.8 and 70.3 to 68.3; DeepSeek-V3-0324 66.7 to 64.2 and 67.1 to 64.4. The authors conclude the difference "is not substantial in our setup". Position bias itself persisted: judged in the original and in the swapped order, the models kept the same verdict only 83.3% to 89.6% of the time (Table 5, direct prompting).
- **Implication for /frontend:** Keep the both-orders rule in the acceptance step (Layer 2), because an acceptance rule wants fewer false accepts and the rule turns an order-dependent verdict into "no improvement". Do not count it as a way to make the judge more accurate, and do not claim it removes position bias. Log the rate of inconsistent pairs per round: a high rate means the two candidates are too close for a judge to separate (F06), and the decision falls to the deterministic gates or to the owner.
- **Evidence:**
  - [WebDevJudge](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18560v3) — Chunyang Li et al.; arXiv HTML v3, 3 Mar 2026; Appendix E.1 and Table 13; accessed 2026-09-30 — peer-reviewed paper (ICLR 2026).
    - "This method involves evaluating each pair of implementations twice, with their positions swapped in the second evaluation. A preference is considered final only if the model's choice remains consistent across both orderings. If the choice is inconsistent, the outcome is recorded as a tie."
    - Table 13 (w/ mitigating: direct, Likert; w/o mitigating: direct, Likert): "Claude-4-sonnet 68.6 68.3 70.0 70.2 ; GPT-4.1 68.8 68.3 68.7 70.3 ; DeepSeekV3-0324 64.2 64.4 66.7 67.1"
    - "The results in Table 13 indicate that the overall performance difference before and after debiasing is not substantial in our setup."
    - Table 5 (consistency, direct): "Claude-4-sonnet 89.6 ... GPT-4.1 83.3 ... DeepSeek-V3-0324 84.1"
- **Limits / false positives:** One benchmark of working web apps where function outweighs looks, expert labels that include ties, three models. Agreement with labels that include ties is not the false-acceptance rate an acceptance rule cares about; no study measured that rate for UI candidates.

### FD17-F29 — Playwright's own docs discourage waiting for "networkidle" in tests; readiness must come from explicit checks (added at verification)
- **Grade:** A · **Kind:** OBSERVED · **Verdict:** VERIFIED (added at verification)
- **Claim:** Playwright's API parameter docs mark the `waitUntil`/`state` value `'networkidle'` as "DISCOURAGED": it waits "until there are no network connections for at least `500` ms" and the docs say "Don't use this method for testing, rely on web assertions to assess readiness instead." By that definition a page that keeps a connection open or polls more often than every 500 ms never reaches it, and a page that goes quiet early can pass it before late content arrives.
- **Implication for /frontend:** The QA script (Deliverable B, step 3) loads with `waitUntil: "load"` and then waits on explicit readiness: `document.fonts.ready`, the hero heading visible, every above-the-fold `img` complete, and, after the scroll pass, no image still loading. Hackathon demos that stream on-chain or market data are exactly the pages that never go network-idle.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Playwright docs: params.md](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/blob/HEAD/docs/src/api/params.md) — Microsoft; fetched raw from `raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/playwright/HEAD/docs/src/api/params.md`; accessed 2026-09-30 — official docs.
    - "`'networkidle'` - **DISCOURAGED** consider operation to be finished when there are no network connections for at least `500` ms. Don't use this method for testing, rely on web assertions to assess readiness instead."
- **Limits / false positives:** The docs give the rule, not a measurement of how often network-idle waits mislead; the explicit checks above still need a timeout.

## Deliverable A — Scoring rubric (proposed)

The rubric has four layers. Layer 0 is binary and deterministic; Layer 1 is a 100-point score used to compare versions and to report to the owner; Layer 2 is the rule that decides whether a revision replaces the current version; Layer 3 lists the human checkpoints. Weights and anchors are my synthesis from the findings, not measured values; every threshold without an external source is marked "calibrate" and must be tuned so that it stays silent on the human control corpus and fires on the owner's past frontends (the /prose method).

### Layer 2 — Acceptance rule for each revision

1. A critic round produces at most 5 issues, each with a mark ID, the measured problem, and the concrete change; items without these are dropped (F02, F11).
2. Three independent critic passes in fresh contexts; an issue is kept if >= 2 passes raise it or a deterministic check confirms it (F15). Inputs: marked viewport tiles (<= 20 images per call), the section source, the element metadata, the rubric slice for that dimension (F03, F04, F07, F08).
3. After the fix, all Layer 0 gates rerun; any regression rejects the candidate (F10, F13).
4. The pairwise judge compares incumbent and candidate on the affected section in both orders; the candidate wins only if preferred in both; otherwise "no improvement" (F14). This is a guard against false accepts, not an accuracy gain: the one measurement found no agreement improvement from the swap (F28). Log the inconsistency rate.
5. Replace the incumbent only if 3 and 4 hold and no Layer 1 deterministic sub-score drops (F10). Otherwise revert to the best-so-far snapshot and try a different fix (at most 2 retries per issue).
6. Stop after 3 rounds per checkpoint, or after 2 consecutive rejected candidates (F09, F10, F05). Pixel-diff the tiles against the previous round and flag changes outside the edited section (pixelmatch threshold 0.2, F27).

### Layer 3 — Human checkpoints (the only places the owner is asked)

- H1 Direction: after reference capture, the owner chooses among 2 or 3 clearly different directions (hero comps or style tiles), because judges are near chance on close options and people invent answers when options are too similar (F06, F16, F18).
- H2 Logo: the owner chooses the mark among candidates rendered at favicon and header sizes (F16; production details in technique-logo-favicon-brand.md).
- H3 Final sign-off before any recording: the owner sees the QA report (gates, score, tiles), because the best measured judges still trail humans by about 14 points (F07) and critique quality falls as the page improves (F05).

## Contradictions between sources

- **How good are judges?** ArtifactsBench reports up to 90.95% pairwise agreement (F08), WebDevJudge a ceiling near 70% (F07), Luera et al. about 60% overall and near chance for small differences (F06), UIClip 51.58% for GPT-4V (F01), TASTE below 0.55 for open VLMs on graphic design (F16). The spread is consistent with Luera's finding that accuracy depends on how different the two items are: ArtifactsBench compares outputs of different models on the same task, WebDevJudge includes ties and functionally equivalent pairs, TASTE compares strong generators on taste. Treat the high number as the best case for clearly different candidates.
- **Secondary misreport within one group.** ReLook's appendix (Tencent, sharing authors with ArtifactsBench) states that ArtifactsBench "conducted a controlled human study with expert web developers evaluating a stratified sample of 200 tasks", with "over 90% agreement (Cohen's κ > 0.85)" and "Spearman ρ = 0.87 for Gemini-2.5-Pro". ArtifactsBench v2 itself reports 280 queries and 6 models, Pair ACC up to 90.95% and 94.4% normalized Footrule; its only kappa (>= 0.8) concerns checklist annotators, and the word "Spearman" does not occur in its text (checked by search on the v2 HTML). Cite ArtifactsBench, not ReLook's summary of it.
- **Does self-revision help?** Critic loops with an external judge and best-of selection gain (F09, F10, F13), while self-revision without an external signal gives little or regresses (Design2Code: Claude 3.5 Sonnet 75.1 to 71.9 block-match; Huang et al.; F12), and LLM feedback gets worse as the design improves (F05). Reconciled by the acceptance rule: external deterministic gates plus pairwise acceptance plus best-so-far.
- **More images or fewer?** ArtifactsBench: several screenshots raised agreement (F08). WebDevJudge: in single-answer grading, adding the screenshot to code lowered Claude-4-Sonnet from 61.77 to 59.17 (F07). AMUSER (arXiv 2609.22971, 19 Sep 2026): "providing visual inputs during recommendation generation can modestly degrade quality". Screenshots help judging comparisons; critique generation may do better on structured DOM data plus a few marked tiles.
- **Whose taste?** Crowd raters judged GPT-4V pages better designed than the (placeholder-stripped) reference web pages 64% of the time, with low rater agreement (F12), and first-impression research favours prototypical, simple pages (F18), while designers ranked Claude Opus 5 pages lowest in a blind study (F17) and designer-majority taste is not predicted by off-the-shelf judges (F16). A judge tuned to crowd preference may reward the generic look the owner wants to eradicate.
- **Direction of the spacing tell.** The owner's tell 15 says AI spacing is inconsistent; an August 2026 measurement says AI pages adhere to the grid more than award-winning human pages and are indistinguishable on spacing rhythm (F21); sibling dossiers record the same split (FD10-F27, FD14-F14).
- **Does swapping the order help?** MT-Bench proposes judging twice with the order swapped as the conservative fix for position bias (F14); WebDevJudge measured it and found agreement with experts unchanged or slightly lower (F28). Both can hold: the swap makes verdicts more conservative without making them more accurate.
- **Papers against their own tables.** Luera et al.'s discussion gives exact Likert accuracy of 48% and 40% while their Table 3 gives 38% and 37% (F06); ReLook's text calls Forced Optimization "the largest impact" (+2.0) while its ablation table shows the vision reward adding +3.3 (F10). Numbers in this dossier are taken from the tables.
- **Verbosity.** ArtifactsBench reports the relation between judged score and response length as "broadly positive" and MT-Bench documents verbosity bias, while designers penalised pages that ran long (F17). A judge may reward exactly the length the jury dislikes; B7 counters it.
- **Nielsen's heuristics.** NN/g recommends them as the default set for heuristic evaluation, while UIClip rejected them for single screenshots and GPT-4 performed worse with them on static mockups (F19).
- **Versions of the Show HN slop study.** Secondary reports of 22 Apr 2026 ([The Agent Times](https://theagenttimes.com/articles/two-thirds-of-show-hn-landing-pages-now-carry-ai-design-fing-80ae1c43), [daily.dev summary](https://preview.app.daily.dev/posts/show-hn-submissions-tripled-and-now-mostly-have-the-same-vibe-coded-look-fj9vjxs5r), both read via Exa search results) cite 500 pages and 21% with 5 or more of 15 patterns; the current [blog post](https://www.adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/) (Adrian Krebs, dated 2026-04-20, fetched with curl) says "I scored 1,590 Show HN submissions ... High (4+ patterns): 22% (347 sites)" out of "the 16 patterns"; the repository README says 14 patterns. The post was revised; cite the current post and the repository (F27).

## Verification notes

**Method.** Every source cited in F01 to F27 and in the contradictions section was re-opened on 2026-09-30. arXiv: the abstract pages of all 19 papers were fetched for titles, authors, version dates and comments, and the HTML full texts (UIClip v1, UICrit v3, AesEval v1, Duan v1, Luera v1, WebDevJudge v3, ArtifactsBench v2, Sansford v1, ReLook v1, Design2Code v3, WebGen-Agent v1, TASTE v3, AMUSER v1, Set-of-Mark v2, Panickssery v1, PoLL v2, Huang v2) were converted to text; the Self-Refine v2 and MT-Bench v4 PDFs and the Tuch preprint were read with `pdftotext`. Each quote was checked with a script that normalises whitespace, curly quotes, dashes and markup, and tables were re-read cell by cell where a number was used. Repositories were read with `gh api` and `raw.githubusercontent.com` (UICrit, AIM, Carbon, Tailwind, axe-core, Playwright, MDN content, devtools-protocol, apca-w3, design-slop-cop, Lost Pixel, pa11y, BackstopJS, Lighthouse), registry facts with `npm view`, and the Hugging Face model through its API. NN/g (five pages), Lyssna, USWDS, web.dev, WCAG 2.2, the WCAG 3.0 draft, Contra Labs, the convergence page and Adrian Krebs's post were fetched with curl; Material Design 3, the CHI 2015 abstract, the KeAi abstract and the two secondary Show HN reports were read through Exa. Venues were checked on ML Anthology (WebDevJudge), the ACL Anthology (ReLook), the NeurIPS proceedings (Self-Refine), Semantic Scholar (Self-Refine, Panickssery) and Crossref (Tuch, the KeAi article).

**What changed.** 12 findings verified as written, 14 corrected, 1 downgraded, 0 dropped, 2 added. The material corrections: F09's code-quality decline was measured only on Distill-Qwen-14B and reversed for Claude 4.5 Sonnet and Haiku; F10's "largest component" contradicts ReLook's own ablation table; F06 had taken exact-match figures from Luera et al.'s discussion that contradict the paper's Table 3; F13's appearance scores come from GPT-4o, not Qwen2.5-VL-32B; the summary's "several screenshots instead of one (79.06% to 90.95%)" started from the no-screenshot condition; the order swap was listed as a measured lever although the only measurement (F28) shows no gain; F01's 51.58% mixes designer-rated and synthetic pairs; F18's blur radii are one illustration, not NN/g's method; F21 now carries grade C because its empirical claim rests on an anonymous page with three AI outputs. F11 was raised from B to A (NeurIPS 2023). Deliverable B step 3 no longer uses `networkidle` (F29), and gate G2 now requires a failed image response.

**Sources that could not be opened.** The full text of the CHI 2015 paper (the IRIS record restricts the PDF to archive managers; only the abstract was read). The full text of the KeAi "Stability without sensitivity" article (DOI resolves to Elsevier; only the abstract via Exa and the Crossref metadata were read). DBLP's record page returned a bot check; the DBLP key comes from Semantic Scholar's response. UIClip's per-dataset accuracies are drawn in Figure 4 as an image and were not read, so GPT-4V's accuracy on the designer-rated BetterApp pairs alone is unknown.

**Residual doubts.** (1) ReLook quotes come from arXiv v1; the ACL 2026 camera-ready may reword the "largest impact" sentence or change the table, and it was not compared. (2) Luera et al. give conflicting numbers for the same metric; the table values are used, but which is right cannot be settled from outside. (3) The selection-bias caveat on F09's "CITL: Best" is an inference from the method ("retains the highest-scoring solution"), not the authors' own statement. (4) The SVG example under F25 illustrates MDN's "does not specify an intrinsic width" case; browser behaviour for such SVGs was not tested. (5) The Panickssery venue (NeurIPS 2024) rests on Semantic Scholar metadata, not the proceedings page. (6) The convergence page (F21) is anonymous and its data and probe, though announced, were not downloaded. (7) Claims that no study exists (MLLM five-second-test validation, logo-specific judge numbers, squint-test validation) were not re-searched at verification.
