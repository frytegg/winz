## Summary

The evidence does not support the popular rule "all duplication is bad, remove it". It also does not support "duplication is fine". Harm is concentrated in identifiable clone groups.

**1. Inconsistent (near-miss, Type-3) clones are where faults hide.**
- **Juergens et al. (ICSE 2009).** Five systems: 3 C#, 1 COBOL, 1 Java.
  - 52% of clone groups were inconsistent, and 28% of those inconsistencies were unintentional.
  - "About every second to third unintentional change to a clone leads to a fault". In total, 107 faults were confirmed by the developers.
  - At least 3–23% of inconsistent clone groups were faulty.
- **Wagner et al. (SANER 2016).** Three automotive Java systems.
  - 17% of Type-3 clone groups contained documented faults, with a range of 3–33%.
  - The authors nevertheless conclude that Type-3 clones are "not a major source of faults in these systems", and link this to developers being aware of the clones.
- **Mondal et al. (ICSME 2017).** Four Java systems.
  - Up to 33% of clone fragments that received bug fixes carried propagated bugs.
  - Most of these were near-miss clones created in the same commit.

**2. Clones that keep having to change together are the second place where harm concentrates.**
- **Kim et al. (ESEC/FSE 2005)**, two Java projects:
  - 36–38% of clone genealogies changed consistently.
  - Of the genealogies that disappeared, 48–72% did so within an average of eight check-ins.
  - 49–64% could not easily be removed with the standard refactorings of the time.

**3. The stability and defect literature conflicts.**
- **Rahman/Bird/Devanbu (EMSE 2012)**, four C projects: clones were not associated with most bugs and "may be less defect prone".
- **Göde & Koschke (ICSE 2011):** most clones rarely change.
- **Harder & Göde (JSEP 2012/2013):** confirmed that cloned code is more stable, while noting that stability varies.
- **Mondal, Rahman, Roy & Schneider (EMSE 2018)**, 12 systems: cloned code was often *more* change-prone. They attribute the earlier disagreement to different tools, systems, setups and aspects of stability.
- **Kapser & Godfrey (EMSE 2008)** rated clones in two C systems at two token thresholds:
  - Apache at 30 tokens: 71% good.
  - Gnumeric at 30 tokens: 57% harmful.
  - Raising the threshold to 60 tokens pushed the harmful share *up* in Apache (to 39%) but *down* in Gnumeric (to 42%). The headline depends on both the system and the threshold.

**4. DRY, as its authors now define it, is about knowledge, not text.** This is the clarification in the Pragmatic Programmer 20th Anniversary Edition: "Not All Code Duplication is Knowledge Duplication". The authors admit they "did a poor job of explaining" DRY in the first edition.

**5. Practitioners are split in the same way.**
- Arguing that duplication is often cheaper than the wrong abstraction:
  - Metz
  - Abramov
  - Dodds (AHA)
  - Google's DAMP guidance for tests
  - the Go proverb "A little copying is better than a little dependency"
- Pushing back:
  - Fowler and Beck
  - the PMD docs
  - several 2026 Hacker News practitioners

**6. Tool defaults differ.**
- jscpd 5.3.2: 50 tokens / 5 lines.
- Sonar: 100 tokens over 10 lines, or 10 statements for Java, which cannot be overridden.
- PMD CPD 7.27.0: `--minimum-tokens` is required.
- pylint 4.0.x: 4 similar lines.
- **Correction:** the formal standard is not silent. OMG ASCMM 1.0 (2016) left `<NumberOfIdenticalTokens>` without a default. Its successor, OMG ASCQM 1.1 (July 2022, still the current version), sets "Limit Volume of Similar Code" (mapped to CWE-1041) at a default of **90% similar elements** between two functions. Its summary table also cites a 10% copy-pasted-instructions default. Neither value is empirically justified in the spec.

**7. The AI-era data conflicts.**
- **GitClear (2025 report).** The report's own multipliers are inconsistent:
  - Its landing page headline says "4x Growth in Code Clones".
  - Its narrative says "an 8-fold increase ... during 2024".
  - It also says duplicate blocks were "approximately 10x higher" than two years earlier.
  - Its table, however, gives the share of commits containing a duplicate block as 0.45% (2022), 1.80% (2023) and 6.66% (2024). That is about 3.7x in 2024 and about 15x over two years.
- **GitClear (2026 page):** block duplication rose 81% from 2023 to 2026.
- **Attribution:** GitClear infers the AI effect from adoption timing. It does not attribute individual lines to AI.
- **Mao et al. (arXiv 2603.27130, 2026):** using jscpd, human-written code was more duplicated than likely-AI code (27.57% vs 14.29% duplicated lines).
- **Ji et al. (arXiv 2607.01867, 2026):** found "substantial intra-repository code clones" in code detected as LLM-generated.

**What the skill takes from this.**
- **Triage procedure.** First exclude generated, vendored and data files. Then give each clone group one of three outcomes:
  - **MAKE CONSISTENT:** the copies differ in a way that may be a latent bug. This changes behaviour, so it needs a test or owner sign-off.
  - **MERGE:** the copies encode the same knowledge, have changed together, can be parameterised without side effects, and sit close together.
  - **LEAVE AND DOCUMENT:** the similarity is coincidental, the requirements are independent, the copies cross a boundary or are diverging, or a merge would need selector flags or hierarchy changes.
- **Pin behaviour first.** Capture current behaviour with tests before any merge.
- **Security fixes.** Make sure every security fix reached every clone.
- **CI gate.** Gate only on *new* duplication.

## Findings

### T08-F02 — Treat inconsistent near-miss (Type-3) and renamed (Type-2) clone groups as possible latent bugs
- **Grade:** A · **Verdict:** VERIFIED
  - All quotes and figures were confirmed in the full texts.
  - Juergens' own caveat on the fault-density comparison was added.
  - The PMD command now uses the documented `--dir` flag. PMD's identifier and literal normalisation applies to Java and C++ only.
- **Rule:**
  - For each inconsistent clone group, diff the instances. Look for a fix, guard or error path that exists in only one copy:
    - null or bounds checks
    - exception handling
    - transaction rollback
    - validation or escaping
    - log level
    - a missing bug fix
  - If one copy was patched in a bug-fix commit and its sibling was not, flag the sibling as a probable defect.
  - Apply the missing change only with a test that demonstrates the defect, or with owner confirmation.
- **Rationale:**
  - Studies of industrial C#, COBOL and Java code, and of OSS Java, find faults inside inconsistent clone groups:
    - **Juergens:** at least 3–23% of inconsistent groups were faulty. The fault density was 3.4–91.4 faults/kLOC in inconsistent lines, against a literature range of 0.1–50. The authors themselves call this comparison "not perfect", because system-wide fault densities were unavailable.
    - **Wagner:** 17% of Type-3 groups had documented faults. They also note that "type-3 clones ... often occur as a result from fixing faults in type-2 clones".
    - **Mondal:** bug propagation happened mainly in near-miss clones.
  - Exact-match detectors miss these groups by construction.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Do Code Clones Matter?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05472) — Juergens, Deissenboeck, Hummel, Wagner (TU München), ICSE 2009 (arXiv upload 2017) — peer_reviewed
    - "About half of the clones (52%) contain inconsistencies... over a quarter (28%) has been introduced unintentionally"; "at least 3-23% of the inconsistencies actually presented a fault"; "about every second to third unintentional change to a clone leads to a fault"; 107 faults.
    - A category-1 example: "one clone of the affected clone group performs a null-check to prevent a null-pointer dereference whereas the other does not". Other examples: "index-out-of-bounds exceptions, incorrect transaction handling and missing rollbacks".
    - Detection settings for C# and Java: a minimum of 10 statements, edit distance 5, and no crossing of method boundaries. For COBOL the minimum length and edit distance were doubled to 20 and 10.
    - Verification: full text checked (Table 2 and Sections 5–6).
  - [On the Relationship of Inconsistent Software Clones and Faults: An Empirical Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08005) — Wagner, Abdulkhaleq, Kaya, Paar, SANER 2016 — peer_reviewed
    - "Of the type-3 clones, 17 % contain faults. Developers modified most of the type-3 clones simultaneously and thereby fixed half of the faults in type-3 clones consistently."
    - The range is "from 3 % to 33 %". Three automotive Java systems of 253, 332 and 454 KLOC.
    - Verification: full text and arXiv journal_ref checked.
  - [Bug Propagation through Code Cloning: An Empirical Study](https://doi.org/10.1109/icsme.2017.33) — Mondal, Roy, Schneider, ICSME 2017 — peer_reviewed
    - "up to 33% of the clone fragments that experience bug-fix changes can contain propagated bugs... near-miss clones are primarily involved with bug-propagation rather than identical clones... Bug propagation is more likely to occur in the clone fragments that are created in the same commit".
    - Four Java OSS systems.
    - Verification: abstract only (OpenAlex).
  - [jscpd Rust engine documentation](https://github.com/kucherenko/jscpd/blob/master/docs/rust.md) and [README](https://github.com/kucherenko/jscpd) — jscpd project, v5.3.2, 2026 — official_docs
    - `--max-gap-lines N` "merges clones of the same file pair whose fragments follow each other in *both* files with at most `N` unmatched lines in between". The result is reported as `similar`.
    - `--ignore-identifiers` / `--ignore-literals` find blocks "reported as `renamed`".
    - `--similarity 0.85` "compares whole JavaScript/TypeScript functions by syntax-tree structure".
    - `--kind` accepts `exact, renamed, similar, gap, ast`.
    - Verification: raw docs fetched, and 5.3.2 confirmed as npm `latest`.
- **Detect:**
  - jscpd: `jscpd src --max-gap-lines 3 --ignore-identifiers --ignore-literals --kind renamed,gap --reporters json`. For JS/TS, add `--similarity 0.85` and include `ast` in `--kind`.
  - Java or C++ via PMD: `pmd cpd --minimum-tokens 100 --ignore-identifiers --ignore-literals --dir src`.
  - For each group, diff the extracted fragments with `git diff --no-index`.
  - To compare history, run `git log --format=%H -L <start>,<end>:<file>` for each instance. Compare the commit sets and read the messages of the fix commits.
- **Tools:**
  - jscpd 5.3.2 (`--max-gap-lines`, `--ignore-identifiers`, `--similarity`, `--kind`)
  - PMD CPD 7.27.0 (`--ignore-identifiers` and `--ignore-literals`, Java and C++ only)
  - `git log -L`
  - `git diff --no-index`
- **Applies to:** All languages. The highest yield is in code that was copy-pasted and then edited: handlers, controllers, data-access code, UI dialogs, validation.
- **When not to apply / counterpoints:**
  - Most inconsistencies are intentional. In Juergens it was "about three quarters". In the COBOL system, whose maintainers were clone-aware, 90% were intentional.
  - Wagner concludes that Type-3 clones "are not a major source of faults in these systems".
  - Göde & Koschke (ICSE 2011): "the number of unintentional inconsistent changes to clones is small".
  - Expect false alarms. Never "fix" a difference without evidence that it is unintended.
- **Fix risk:** high

### T08-F03 — Do not deduplicate to hit a duplication-% target; do not treat duplication % as a defect proxy
- **Grade:** A · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
  - Md Saidur Rahman was missing from the Mondal 2018 author list.
  - The Kapser threshold effect was shown to depend on the system.
  - The unconfirmed "MSR 2010 best paper" label on Rahman et al. was removed. It is not in the paper and could not be confirmed on the authors' pages.
- **Rule:**
  - On a finished project, act only on clone groups selected by triage (F01, F02, F08).
  - Use the global duplication percentage only as a trend indicator and as a gate on new code (F12).
- **Rationale:**
  - Peer-reviewed studies disagree on whether cloned code is more defect-prone or more change-prone.
  - Harm is concentrated in a minority of clone groups.
  - Every merge risks changing behaviour and creating a wrong abstraction (F06, F07).
  - A percentage target spends that risk on clones that are mostly harmless.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Clones: what is that smell?](https://cabird.com/pdfs/rahman2012cwt.pdf) — F. Rahman, C. Bird, P. Devanbu, Empirical Software Engineering 17(4-5):503–530, 2012 (online Dec 2011; extended from their MSR 2010 paper) — peer_reviewed
    - "the great majority of bugs are not significantly associated with clones... clones may be less defect prone than non-cloned code... little evidence that clones with more copies are actually more error prone".
    - Four C projects (Apache httpd, Nautilus, Evolution, Gimp), analysed with Deckard at a 50-token minimum.
    - Verification: full text and Crossref checked.
  - [Frequency and risks of changes to clones](https://doi.org/10.1145/1985793.1985836) — N. Göde, R. Koschke, ICSE 2011 — peer_reviewed
    - "most clones are rarely changed and the number of unintentional inconsistent changes to clones is small. We thus have to carefully select the clones to be managed to avoid unnecessary effort managing clones with no risk potential."
    - Verification: abstract only (OpenAlex), venue checked with Crossref.
  - [Is cloned code really stable?](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-017-9528-y) — M. Mondal, **Md S. Rahman**, C. K. Roy, K. A. Schneider, Empirical Software Engineering 23(2):693–770, 2018 — peer_reviewed
    - 12 systems (Java, C, C#), NiCad and CCFinderX: "cloned code is often more unstable (change-prone) than non-cloned code in the maintenance phase, (ii) both Type 1 and Type 3 clones appear to exhibit higher instability than Type 2 clones".
    - They also "replicated the original studies with their original settings and found mostly equivalent results", which suggests the conflict is driven by method.
    - Verification: abstract only (Springer metadata). Authors confirmed via Springer and OpenAlex.
  - [Cloned code: stable code](https://doi.org/10.1002/smr.1551) — J. Harder, N. Göde, J. Software: Evolution and Process 25(10), 2013 (online 2012) — peer_reviewed
    - "In general, we were able to confirm the findings of the previous study. Nevertheless, our results also show that clone stability varies depending on the clones' characteristics, the corresponding project environment, and over time."
    - Verification: abstract only (OpenAlex), volume confirmed with Crossref.
  - ["Cloning considered harmful" considered harmful](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~migod/papers/2008/emse08-ClonePatterns.pdf) — C. Kapser, M. Godfrey, Empirical Software Engineering 13(6):645–692, 2008 — peer_reviewed
    - Apache at 30 tokens: "71% of the 59 true positive RGCs were considered good and only 14% were considered harmful". At 60 tokens: "42% of the clones were considered good and 39% were considered harmful".
    - Gnumeric at 30 tokens: "57% of the RGCs sampled were considered harmful". At 60 tokens, 42% (30 of 71).
    - Verification: full text and Tables 3–6 checked.
- **Detect:** Apply a policy check to the proposed change set. Reject a dedupe plan driven by a global percentage or by "all detector hits" instead of per-group triage. Require a triage record for each merged group.
- **Tools:** `jscpd --history` (trend only); SonarQube `duplicated_lines_density` (trend only).
- **Applies to:** All languages; hardening passes on finished projects.
- **When not to apply / counterpoints:**
  - Juergens 2009 and Mondal 2017/2018 show real costs.
  - GitClear (2026) argues that "A duplicated block imposes a propagation tax".
  - The evidence is mostly Java, C, C# and COBOL from 2005–2018, with little on TypeScript, Python or agent-maintained code.
- **Fix risk:** low

### T08-F07 — Merge only when differences parameterise without side effects; prefer close Type-1/2 clones; avoid hierarchy refactorings
- **Grade:** A · **Verdict:** VERIFIED. A caveat was added that Kim et al.'s "unfactorable" reflects Java language limits of 2005. The advice to pin behaviour with characterisation tests is general practice, not from these sources.
- **Rule:**
  - Before merging, establish that the differences can be parameterised without side effects. These must stay identical:
    - argument evaluation order
    - exception types and timing
    - mutation of shared state
    - early returns
    - resource cleanup
    - performance-sensitive paths
  - Prefer merges of Type-1/Type-2 clones that sit close together (same method, class, file or module).
  - Avoid merges that need class-hierarchy refactorings (Pull Up Method, Extract Superclass, Template Method) unless every subclass is well covered by tests.
  - Pin current behaviour with characterisation tests first.
- **Rationale:**
  - Refactorability depends strongly on clone properties, and many long-lived clones cannot be merged with standard refactorings.
  - Refactorings do induce faults, and hierarchy refactorings do so "very frequently".
- **Evidence:**
  - [Assessing the Refactorability of Software Clones](https://doi.org/10.1109/tse.2015.2448531) — N. Tsantalis, D. Mazinanian, G. P. Krishnan, IEEE TSE, 2015 — peer_reviewed
    - The approach checks "if the differences present between the clones can be safely parameterized without causing any side-effects".
    - Over a million clone pairs, four detectors, nine projects: "(a) clones in production code tend to be more refactorable than clones in test code, (b) clones with a close relative location (i.e., same method, type, or file) tend to be more refactorable... (c) Type-1 clones tend to be more refactorable... (d) clones with a small size tend to be more refactorable".
    - Verification: abstract only (OpenAlex).
  - [When Does a Refactoring Induce Bugs? An Empirical Study](https://doi.org/10.1109/scam.2012.20) — G. Bavota, B. De Carluccio, A. De Lucia, M. Di Penta, R. Oliveto, O. Strollo, SCAM 2012 — peer_reviewed
    - 15,008 refactorings of 52 kinds in Ant, Xerces and ArgoUML, with fault attribution by SZZ: "refactorings involving hierarchies (e.g., pull up method), tend to induce faults very frequently."
    - Verification: abstract only (OpenAlex).
  - [An Empirical Study of Code Clone Genealogies](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~miryung/Publications/esecfse05-clonegenealogy.pdf) — M. Kim, V. Sazawal, D. Notkin, G. Murphy, ESEC/FSE 2005 — peer_reviewed
    - "49% to 64% of clone genealogies consist of clones that cannot be easily removed using standard refactoring techniques. In fact, the longer code clones survive in the system, the more they represent this type of unfactorable clones".
    - Two Java projects (carol, dnsjava). Unfactorability is attributed to "programming language limitations".
    - Verification: full text checked.
  - [JDeodorant: Clone Refactoring Support beyond IDEs](http://blog.ieeesoftware.org/2016/10/jdeodorant-clone-refactoring-support.html) — N. Tsantalis (Concordia), IEEE Software Blog, 16 Oct 2016 — engineering_blog
    - "Software clones tend to diverge after the initial copy-and-paste"; IDEs "support the refactoring of clones with trivial differences"; JDeodorant examines "whether the parameterization of the differences appearing in the clones is free of side effects".
    - Verification: page fetched.
- **Detect:**
  - For each candidate, list every difference token by token, from the detector output plus a diff.
  - Classify each difference as a literal, identifier, expression, call or statement gap.
  - Reject the merge if any difference is a statement gap with side effects, involves different exception or cleanup paths, or needs a new class hierarchy.
  - Run the full test suite and coverage before and after the merge. Confirm that coverage of each former instance's callers did not drop.
  - Java: use JDeodorant's refactorability analysis.
- **Tools:**
  - JDeodorant (Java/Eclipse)
  - IDE Extract Method / Inline Method
  - coverage: c8/istanbul, coverage.py, JaCoCo
  - mutation testing for high-risk merges: Stryker, mutmut, PIT
- **Applies to:** All languages, especially OO code where merges go through inheritance.
- **When not to apply / counterpoints:**
  - All three empirical sources cover Java only.
  - Modern generics and lambdas make more clones mergeable, so the 49–64% figure should not be transferred to current languages.
  - Bavota's SZZ-based fault attribution is noisy.
  - HN user bazoom42 (2026, [48620440](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48620440)) argues the opposite asymmetry: an abstraction that is only indirection is easy to inline, while consolidating duplicates that have drifted apart is "pretty difficult and error prone".
- **Fix risk:** high

### T08-F13 — Security hardening: every security fix must reach every clone
- **Grade:** A · **Verdict:** VERIFIED. The researcher's paraphrase of CWE-1041 was replaced with the verbatim text.
- **Rule:**
  - For each security-relevant commit in history (CVE, auth, access control, escaping or sanitising, crypto, injection), search the whole repository for clones of the pre-fix fragment. Confirm that every copy received the fix.
  - Do the same for every snippet copied from third-party code.
  - Record the origin and version of each copied snippet so it can be tracked like a dependency.
- **Rationale:**
  - Clones are a documented channel through which vulnerabilities propagate, and large-scale scans have found thousands of unpatched copies.
  - MITRE's CWE-1041 names the failure mode: fixing one copy and forgetting another.
- **Evidence:**
  - [ReDeBug: Finding Unpatched Code Clones in Entire OS Distributions](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp.2012.13) — J. Jang, A. Agrawal, D. Brumley, IEEE S&P 2012 — peer_reviewed
    - "Programmers should never fix the same bug twice... found 15,546 unpatched copies of known vulnerable code in currently deployed code by checking 376 Debian/Ubuntu security-related patches... confirming 145 real bugs".
    - Verification: abstract only (OpenAlex).
  - [VUDDY: A Scalable Approach for Vulnerable Code Clone Discovery](https://doi.org/10.1109/sp.2017.62) — S. Kim, S. Woo, H. Lee, H. Oh, IEEE S&P 2017 — peer_reviewed
    - "vulnerabilities and bugs can easily be propagated through code clones"; it detected "zero-day vulnerabilities in widely used software systems, such as Apache HTTPD and Ubuntu OS Distribution".
    - Verification: abstract only (OpenAlex).
  - [CWE-1041: Use of Redundant Code](https://cwe.mitre.org/data/definitions/1041.html) — MITRE, CWE 4.20 — standard
    - "...indirectly affects security by making it more difficult or time-consuming to find and/or fix vulnerabilities. For example, if there are two copies of the same code, the programmer might fix a weakness in one copy while forgetting to fix the same weakness in another copy."
    - Mitigation: "Merge common functionality into a single function and then call that function from across the entire code base."
    - Verification: page fetched (version 4.20 shown).
- **Detect:**
  - List security commits: `git log -i -E --grep='CVE-|security|vuln|xss|csrf|inject|saniti|escape|auth' --format='%h %s'`.
  - For each hit, recover the pre-fix fragment with `git show <sha>^:<file>`. Put it temporarily in the tree and run `jscpd --ignore-identifiers --min-tokens 30`. Alternatively, `rg` for its distinctive identifiers and API calls.
  - Find pasted third-party code with `rg -n 'stackoverflow.com|copied from|adapted from'`.
- **Tools:**
  - `git log --grep`
  - jscpd `--ignore-identifiers`
  - ripgrep
  - Semgrep/CodeQL for the vulnerable API pattern
- **Applies to:** All languages. High priority for auth, parsing, serialisation, crypto and input handling.
- **When not to apply / counterpoints:**
  - CWE-1041 has mapping status "PROHIBITED": "primarily a quality issue with no direct security implications". Duplication amplifies vulnerabilities but is not one itself.
  - ReDeBug and VUDDY operate at OS-distribution scale on mostly C/C++ code. How much this finds inside a single application repository has not been measured.
- **Fix risk:** high

### T08-F11 — Configure detection so the report is trustworthy, and always state the threshold
- **Grade:** A · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
  - The claim that "the formal standard fixes no threshold" was outdated. OMG ASCQM 1.1 (2022) supersedes ASCMM 1.0 and sets defaults.
  - The Kapser threshold effect depends on the system.
  - pylint is now 4.0.9, with the default unchanged. The pylint value N=8 is flagged as unsourced.
  - The jscpd "51.30% vs 3.37%" example was a jscpd statistics bug fixed in 5.3.2. It does not show that mixed-format scans inflate numbers by an order of magnitude.
- **Rule:**
  - Use a language-aware, token-based detector with a minimum of about 50–100 tokens. Defaults: jscpd 50 tokens and 5 lines; Sonar 100 tokens across 10 lines, or 10 statements for Java.
  - Raise pylint's default of 4 lines before acting on its output.
  - Restrict scans to code formats. Exclude generated, vendored, build output, lockfiles, data and markup.
  - When hunting inconsistent clones, keep matches within function boundaries.
  - Always state the threshold next to any duplication number.
- **Rationale:**
  - Tools disagree on defaults.
  - The threshold changes which clones you see, and even their harmful-versus-good profile.
  - Counting non-code text distorts the percentages.
- **Evidence:**
  - [jscpd Rust engine docs, CLI reference](https://github.com/kucherenko/jscpd/blob/master/docs/rust.md) — jscpd project, v5.3.2 — official_docs
    - "`--min-tokens` | `-k` | Minimum tokens in a clone | 50"; "`--min-lines` | `-l` | Minimum lines in a clone | 5".
    - "`--mode mild` (default) drops whitespace tokens, `--mode weak` also drops comments, `--mode strict` keeps every token".

### T08-F01 — Triage every clone group with a decision procedure; never blanket-deduplicate
- **Grade:** B · **Verdict:** VERIFIED
- **Rule:** Work through the steps in order.
  - **(0) EXCLUDE** generated, vendored, lockfile, markup/data and fixture code.
  - **(1) MAKE CONSISTENT FIRST** when the instances differ in an unintended way: a check, error path, rollback, log level, security check or bug fix present in only one copy. This is a behaviour change, so it needs a failing test or owner confirmation.
  - **(2) MERGE** only when all of these hold:
    - (a) the copies encode the same knowledge, meaning one requirement change would force editing every copy;
    - (b) history shows they changed together, or received the same fix;
    - (c) the differences can be parameterised without side effects;
    - (d) the copies are close together, and the merge needs no new selector flag and no class-hierarchy change.
  - **(3) LEAVE AND DOCUMENT** otherwise:
    - coincidental similarity
    - independent external requirements
    - separately deployed or owned components
    - clones that are already diverging
    - merges that need boolean or mode parameters, or a pull-up into a superclass
  - Co-locate, cross-reference and suppress each kept group (F15).
- **Rationale:**
  - Harm concentrates in inconsistent and co-changing groups (F02, F08).
  - Many clones are harmless, short-lived or not refactorable (F03, F07).
  - A wrong merge creates a wrong abstraction (F06).
  - The procedure itself is a synthesis of these findings, hence grade B.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Do Code Clones Matter?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05472) — Juergens et al., ICSE 2009 — peer_reviewed
    - "About half of the clones (52%) contain inconsistencies... over a quarter (28%) has been introduced unintentionally"; "about every second to third unintentional change to a clone leads to a fault"; 107 faults in five systems (C#, Java, COBOL).
    - Verification: full text checked.
  - ["Cloning considered harmful" considered harmful](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~migod/papers/2008/emse08-ClonePatterns.pdf) — Kapser & Godfrey, EMSE 13(6), 2008 — peer_reviewed
    - "If the cloned code acts primarily as an interface to independent external systems, then the requirements of the cloned code segments are less likely to evolve together". Clones involving "concepts internal to the system... will need to evolve together".
    - Verification: full text checked.
  - [The Pragmatic Programmer, 20th Anniversary Edition, Topic 9 "The Evils of Duplication" (publisher excerpt)](https://media.pragprog.com/titles/tpp20/dry.pdf) — D. Thomas, A. Hunt, Pragmatic Bookshelf / Pearson, 2019 (© 2020) — book
    - "Here's the acid test: when some single facet of the code has to change, do you find yourself making that change in multiple places, and in multiple different formats?... If so, your code isn't DRY." / "Not All Code Duplication is Knowledge Duplication".
    - Verification: official excerpt PDF checked.
  - [Assessing the Refactorability of Software Clones](https://doi.org/10.1109/tse.2015.2448531) — Tsantalis, Mazinanian, Krishnan, IEEE TSE 2015 — peer_reviewed
    - The approach checks "if the differences present between the clones can be safely parameterized without causing any side-effects".
    - Clones in close locations, Type-1 clones and small clones are more refactorable; test-code clones are less so.
    - Verification: abstract only (OpenAlex).
- **Detect:**
  - Run a detector (F11) and export JSON.
  - For each group, record:
    - locations
    - clone kind (exact, renamed or similar)
    - a line diff of the instances
    - the co-change count from git (F08)
  - Assign MERGE, MAKE-CONSISTENT, LEAVE or EXCLUDE, with a written reason. A group is ready for action only when every question in the rule has an answer.
- **Tools:**
  - jscpd 5.3.2
  - PMD CPD 7.27.0
  - SonarQube Server
  - `git log -L`
  - diff
- **Applies to:** All languages; a finished, working codebase with tests and git history.
- **When not to apply / counterpoints:**
  - Fowler, [Beck Design Rules](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/BeckDesignRules.html) (2 Mar 2015): "the exercise of eliminating duplication is a powerful way to drive out good designs", and "adding duplication to increase clarity is often papering over a problem".
  - PMD docs: "the most viable solution is to not duplicate".
  - The procedure needs judgement, and reviewers may triage the same group differently.
- **Fix risk:** medium

### T08-F08 — Use change history as the merge trigger; fall back to the rule of three
- **Grade:** B · **Verdict:** VERIFIED. A note was added that Glass's "rule of three" concerns reuse libraries across applications, not in-code clones.
- **Rule:**
  - Merge clone groups whose instances were edited together in two or more commits, or where the same fix was applied to several copies.
  - Leave groups that were never touched after creation, and young two-instance clones that are still diverging.
  - When history is thin, fall back to the rule of three: two similar instances can stay, and a third triggers extraction.
- **Rationale:**
  - A clone costs something only when it must change.
  - About a third of genealogies change consistently, and these are the maintenance burden. Many young clones disappear quickly on their own.
  - Repeated identical edits are the clearest evidence of shared knowledge.
- **Evidence:**
  - [An Empirical Study of Code Clone Genealogies](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~miryung/Publications/esecfse05-clonegenealogy.pdf) — Kim, Sazawal, Notkin, Murphy, ESEC/FSE 2005 — peer_reviewed
    - "36% to 38% of clone genealogies consist of clones that have changed consistently with other elements in the same group".
    - "among the clone genealogies that disappeared during evolution, 48% to 72% disappeared within an average of eight check-ins... aggressive, immediate refactoring may not be necessary or beneficial".
    - Verification: full text checked; two Java projects.
  - [JDeodorant: Clone Refactoring Support beyond IDEs](http://blog.ieeesoftware.org/2016/10/jdeodorant-clone-refactoring-support.html) — N. Tsantalis, IEEE Software Blog, 2016 — engineering_blog
    - "if you find yourself repeatedly applying the same changes (e.g., bug fix) in multiple places in your code, you should definitely consider to refactor this duplication."
    - Verification: page fetched.
  - [Change Coupling: Visualize Logical Dependencies](https://codescene.io/docs/guides/technical/change-coupling.html) — CodeScene docs — official_docs
    - Modules are coupled "if they are modified in the same commit, or if they are modified by the same programmer within a specific period of time, or if they refer to the same Ticket ID in their commit messages". "Change coupling in itself is neither good nor bad".
    - Verification: page fetched.
  - [Rule of three (computer programming)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_of_three_(computer_programming)) — Wikipedia (secondary) — other
    - "two instances of similar code do not require refactoring, but when similar code is used three times, it should be extracted into a new procedure. The rule was popularised by Martin Fowler in Refactoring and attributed to Don Roberts."
    - Verification: fetched via the MediaWiki API. The original book was not accessed.
  - [The Rule of Three](https://blog.codinghorror.com/rule-of-three/) — Jeff Atwood, Coding Horror, 18 Jul 2013 — engineering_blog
    - Quoting Robert Glass (*Facts and Fallacies of Software Engineering*, fact #18): "a reusable component should be tried out in three different applications before it will be sufficiently general to accept into a reuse library."
    - Verification: page fetched. This concerns reuse libraries, so it supports the rule of three only by analogy.
- **Detect:**
  - Per instance: `git log --format=%H -L <start>,<end>:<file> | grep -E '^[0-9a-f]{40}$' | sort > inst_N.txt`. Then `comm -12 inst_1.txt inst_2.txt | wc -l` counts the shared commits.
  - File level: `comm -12 <(git log --format=%H -- a.ts | sort) <(git log --format=%H -- b.ts | sort) | wc -l`.
  - Compare the subjects of fix commits with `git log -i --grep=fix`.
  - Clone age: `git blame --porcelain`.
- **Tools:**
  - `git log -L`
  - `git blame`
  - `comm`
  - CodeScene change coupling
- **Applies to:** All languages; repositories with meaningful history, not squashed into a single import commit.
- **When not to apply / counterpoints:**
  - The rule of three is folklore, C-level on its own.
  - Kent C. Dodds' [AHA Programming](https://kentcdodds.com/blog/aha-programming) calls the related WET rule "similarly dogmatic and over prescriptive".
  - Co-change is an imperfect proxy. Squash merges, formatting sweeps and renames inflate it, and finished projects may have little post-release history.
  - HN user dofm (2026, [48620788](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48620788)) says that in practice the copies are already in production and "There is not the time or budget, just copy it again".
- **Fix risk:** low

### T08-F15 — When you keep a clone group, make it safe: co-locate, cross-reference, suppress explicitly
- **Grade:** B · **Verdict:** CORRECTED. The Late Propagation paper is ECEASST vol. 63, **2014** (Proc. IWSC 2014), not 2024. OpenAlex carries a 2024 record date, apparently from a platform migration.
- **Rule:**
  - Co-locate kept instances where possible.
  - Add a cross-reference comment in each copy naming its siblings and why they are separate.

  - Fault risk falls when developers know the clones exist.

    - Six systems in three languages: "late propagation is more intense in Type 3 clones... Block clones are mostly involved in late propagation... If not refactorable, then the clones that often need to be changed together consistently should be placed in close proximity to one another."
    - Verification: the [author PDF](https://www.cs.usask.ca/faculty/croy/papers/2014/MondalIWSC2014LatePro.pdf) header reads "Electronic Communications of the EASST Volume 63 (2014)... IWSC 2014".

### T08-F04 — Apply the DRY test to knowledge, not text
- **Grade:** B · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
  - The Artima quote "every time we changed the database, boom, we just regenerated the layer" was misattributed to Dave Thomas. It is interviewer **Bill Venners**' own anecdote.
  - A note was added that `no-duplicate-string` is off in sonarjs `recommended`.
- **Rule:**
  - Merge only duplicates that express the same piece of knowledge, meaning one requirement change would force editing every copy.
  - Leave coincidental look-alikes. The book's example is `validate_age` and `validate_quantity` with identical bodies.
  - Conversely, hunt knowledge that is duplicated in different forms even when the text differs: the same constant, regex, URL, limit or schema hand-written in several places, such as a DB schema plus a struct, or client plus server validation.
  - Give each such piece of knowledge a single authoritative source: a constant, a shared schema, or code generation.
- **Rationale:**
  - The authors of DRY explicitly reject the "no copy-paste of lines" reading.
  - Merging coincidental similarity couples unrelated requirements.
  - Knowledge scattered across formats is what actually drifts.
- **Evidence:**
  - [The Pragmatic Programmer, 20th Anniversary Edition, Topic 9 (publisher excerpt)](https://media.pragprog.com/titles/tpp20/dry.pdf) — D. Thomas, A. Hunt, 2019 — book
    - "In the first edition of this book we did a poor job of explaining just what we meant by Don't Repeat Yourself. Many people took it to refer to code only... That is part of DRY, but it's a tiny and fairly trivial part. DRY is about the duplication of knowledge, of intent."
    - "The code is the same, but the knowledge they represent is different... That's a coincidence, not a duplication."
    - Verification: official excerpt checked.
  - [Orthogonality and the DRY Principle](https://www.artima.com/articles/orthogonality-and-the-dry-principle) — Bill Venners interviewing Andy Hunt and Dave Thomas, Artima, 10 Mar 2003 — other
    - Dave Thomas: "Most people take DRY to mean you shouldn't duplicate code. That's not its intention... A system's knowledge is far broader than just its code. It refers to database schemas, test plans, the build system, even documentation."
    - On code generation: Andy Hunt calls a generator "an investment" worth making "If you expect a lot of volatility". Bill Venners describes generating the DB layer from the schema: "Every time we changed the database, boom, we just regenerated the layer."
    - Verification: raw HTML read, with each speaker label checked.
  - [HN comment on "Goodbye, Clean Code"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=22022599) — boffinism, 11 Jan 2020 — practitioner_forum
    - "It's not just about whether two bits of code are similar now. It's about whether, when they change in future, they are likely to change in the same ways."
    - Verification: HN API.
  - [eslint-plugin-sonarjs 4.2.1: no-duplicate-string (S1192)](https://www.npmjs.com/package/eslint-plugin-sonarjs) — SonarSource, 2026 — official_docs

    - It is **not** in the `recommended` config and must be enabled explicitly.
    - Verification: tarball README and `cjs/S1192` source read.
- **Detect:**
  - For each clone group, write down the one requirement or business rule it implements. If the copies map to different rules, owners or tickets, the similarity is coincidental.
  - For knowledge duplication:
    - Enable sonarjs `no-duplicate-string`.
    - Find repeated literals, e.g. `rg -n --no-heading -o '"https?://[^"]+"' | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn`.
    - Compare hand-written types and validators with DB and OpenAPI schemas.
- **Tools:**
  - eslint-plugin-sonarjs `no-duplicate-string` (S1192)
  - ripgrep
  - OpenAPI/JSON Schema code generators
- **Applies to:** All languages; also config, schemas, docs and tests.
- **When not to apply / counterpoints:**
  - The knowledge test is subjective.
  - Kapser rated repeated cross-cutting code (access control, logging, debugging) as mostly good: 12 of 13 in Apache at 30 tokens. Gnumeric at 30 tokens was split 2 good, 2 harmful.
  - A shared constant can couple the release cycles of separate services (F14).
- **Fix risk:** medium

### T08-F05 — Prioritise "parameterized code" clones; deprioritise platform, boilerplate and cross-cutting clones
- **Grade:** B · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
  - The 76%/71% figures come from the **Gnumeric** samples only. The researcher presented them as general.
  - The claim that this is "the only study that rated clones by pattern" could not be confirmed and was removed.
  - The pattern's harm rates were added for all four samples.
- **Rule:**
  - Prioritise clones where the same code differs only in the function called or an identifier. Merge them with a parameter or a higher-order function.

  - Deprioritise the patterns that Kapser & Godfrey rated mostly beneficial:
    - platform variation
    - boiler-plating

    - replicate-and-specialize with non-trivial interleaved changes

  - Platform-variation and boiler-plate clones were rated good in Apache: 9/9 and 8/8 at 30 tokens, 10/10 and 5/5 at 60 tokens.

  - ["Cloning considered harmful" considered harmful](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~migod/papers/2008/emse08-ClonePatterns.pdf) — Kapser & Godfrey, EMSE 2008 — peer_reviewed
    - Gnumeric section: "Parameterized code clones were considered harmful 76% of the time in the sample of small clones, and 71% of the time in the sample of large clones.     - Table 3 (Apache, 30 tokens): cross-cutting 12/13 good; replicate-and-specialize 10/13 good.
    - Verification: full text and all four tables checked (raw-mode extraction).
  - [Lobsters comment on "Goodbye, Clean Code"](https://lobste.rs/c/rnuopl) — thev, 12 Jan 2020 — practitioner_forum

### T08-F06 — Detect wrong abstractions and apply inverse refactoring
- **Grade:** C · **Verdict:** CORRECTED
  - Dodds quotes the WET definition while calling WET "similarly dogmatic and over prescriptive". It is not an endorsement.
  - The AHA acronym is credited in his post to Cher Scarlett.
- **Rule:**
  - When a shared function is steered by selector or boolean "mode" parameters, or holds caller-specific branches, do not add another flag.
  - Inline it back into each caller, delete in each caller the paths it never takes, and re-extract only what is truly common.
  - Refuse any dedupe that would need a new selector parameter.
- **Rationale:**
  - Metz documents how an abstraction decays through added parameters and conditionals.
  - Linters encode the same signal as boolean-trap and selector-parameter rules.
  - Abramov's widely cited deduplication had to be reverted.
- **Evidence:**
  - [The Wrong Abstraction](https://sandimetz.com/blog/2016/1/20/the-wrong-abstraction) — Sandi Metz, 20 Jan 2016 — engineering_blog
    - "duplication is far cheaper than the wrong abstraction".
    - "If you find yourself passing parameters and adding conditional paths through shared code, the abstraction is incorrect."
    - "Re-introduce duplication by inlining the abstracted code back into every caller... Delete the bits that aren't needed for this particular caller."
    - Verification: page fetched.
  - [Goodbye, Clean Code](https://overreacted.io/goodbye-clean-code/) — Dan Abramov, 11 Jan 2020 — engineering_blog
    - "My code traded the ability to change requirements for reduced duplication, and it was not a good trade."
    - His boss "politely asked me to revert my change".
    - "we later needed many special cases and behaviors for different handles on different shapes. My abstraction would have to become several times more convoluted to afford that".
    - Verification: raw HTML checked.
  - [Ruff FBT001 boolean-type-hint-positional-argument](https://docs.astral.sh/ruff/rules/boolean-type-hint-positional-argument/) — Astral, ruff 0.16.8 — official_docs
    - "The use of a boolean will also limit the function to only two possible behaviors, which makes the function difficult to extend in the future. Instead, consider refactoring into separate implementations for the `True` and `False` cases, using an `Enum`, or making the argument a keyword-only argument".
    - FBT002 (boolean-default-value-positional-argument) also exists.
    - Verification: pages fetched.
  - [eslint-plugin-sonarjs 4.2.1: no-selector-parameter (S2301)](https://www.npmjs.com/package/eslint-plugin-sonarjs) — SonarSource — official_docs
    - "Methods should not contain selector parameters". It is in `recommended` and needs type information.
    - Verification: tarball README.
  - [AHA Programming](https://kentcdodds.com/blog/aha-programming) — Kent C. Dodds, 22 Jun 2020 — engineering_blog
    - AHA = "Avoid Hasty Abstractions", an acronym credited to Cher Scarlett. "Optimize for change first".
    - On WET, which Dodds calls "similarly dogmatic and over prescriptive", he quotes Conlin Durbin's definition: "You can ask yourself 'Haven't I written this before?' two times, but never three."
    - Verification: page fetched.
- **Detect:**
  - TS/JS: sonarjs `no-selector-parameter`, with typed linting.
  - Python: `ruff check --select FBT001,FBT002`.
  - Manually list shared helpers with more than 3 callers, and count the branches that test a parameter. The signal is a parameter named like `mode|kind|type|variant|isX|withX` that is used only in conditionals.
  - In the helper's git history, each added parameter or branch tied to a single caller's ticket confirms the pattern.
- **Tools:**
  - eslint-plugin-sonarjs S2301
  - Ruff FBT001/FBT002
  - IDE Inline Function
  - `git log -p`
- **Applies to:** All languages; shared utilities, base classes and "common" modules.
- **When not to apply / counterpoints:**
  - HN user dofm (2026, [48620465](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48620465), [48620517](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48620517)) argues that any abstraction you can actively maintain beats duplication, and that "beyond that, any stable abstraction is better than duplicated code" once past "de minimis" reasons. He also says duplication at the wrong scale burns out even junior developers.
  - HN user lokar (2026, [48620711](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48620711)): tool-assisted refactoring made duplicated code "much faster, cheaper and safer" to deal with, while "Changing abstraction is still high risk".
  - Metz herself: "it occasionally makes sense to accumulate a few conditionals to gain insight".
  - A boolean parameter is not always a wrong abstraction.
- **Fix risk:** medium

### T08-F14 — Across ownership or deployment boundaries, prefer a little copying to a new shared dependency
- **Grade:** C · **Verdict:** VERIFIED. A note was added that the Go proverb and Russ Cox are talking about third-party dependencies, so applying them to internal shared libraries is an extrapolation.
- **Rule:**
  - Do not merge clones that live in separately deployed, versioned or owned components into a new shared library just to remove duplication. This covers microservices, independently published packages, per-platform or per-vendor adapters, and plugins.
  - Merge only when the copies must change in lockstep, meaning they share both knowledge and lifecycle.
  - Keep copied code small and record its origin.
- **Rationale:**
  - A shared library turns textual duplication into release and ownership coupling.
  - Kapser rated clones spanning independent external systems as mostly beneficial.
- **Evidence:**
  - [Go Proverbs](https://go-proverbs.github.io/) — Rob Pike, Gopherfest SV 2015 talk — talk
    - "A little copying is better than a little dependency."
    - Verification: page fetched.
  - [Our Software Dependency Problem](https://research.swtch.com/deps) — Russ Cox, 23 Jan 2019 — engineering_blog
    - "If you only need a tiny fraction of a dependency, it may be simplest to make a copy of what you need (preserving appropriate copyright and other legal notices, of course). You are taking on responsibility for fixing bugs, maintenance, and so on, but you're also completely isolated from the larger risks."
    - He follows this with the Go proverb.
    - Verification: page fetched.
  - [Is DRY the enemy of software project management? (answer 328515)](https://softwareengineering.stackexchange.com/questions/328512/is-dry-the-enemy-of-software-project-management) — Doc Brown, SE.SE, Aug 2016 — practitioner_forum
    - "When those two software components have a completely different life cycle, then coupling them together by refactoring common code into a common lib which is itself under heavy development can indeed have unpredictable effects for the effort. On the other hand, when copying code sections inside one program or program system, all these parts will typically have the same life cycle."
    - Verification: Stack Exchange API.
  - ["Cloning considered harmful" considered harmful](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~migod/papers/2008/emse08-ClonePatterns.pdf) — Kapser & Godfrey, 2008 — peer_reviewed
    - Database-driver example (Postgresql, Mysql, Oracle): "each system will add, remove, and change features independently... code cloning is less likely to be harmful and more likely to be beneficial as it enables maintainers to freely evolve the code."

  - jscpd `--skip-local` / `--skip-isolated`
  - CODEOWNERS
  - git co-change analysis

It's (almost) never correct to have duplicate code for the same object."
  - HN user hedora (2026, [48620598](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48620598)): copy-pasting flows "a few levels up" produced "two parallel universes", ending in "massive production dataloss".
  - Contract and schema knowledge should still have one source, for example a generated client (F04).
- **Fix risk:** high

## Contradictions and open questions

**Contradictions**

- **Is cloned code harmful?**
  - Evidence that it is:
    - Juergens 2009: at least 3–23% of inconsistent groups faulty.
    - Mondal 2017: up to 33% of bug-fixed clone fragments carry propagated bugs.
    - Mondal 2018: cloned code more change-prone in 12 systems.
  - Evidence that it is not:
    - Rahman/Bird/Devanbu 2012: clones are not more defect-prone.
    - Göde & Koschke 2011: most clones rarely change.
    - Harder & Göde: cloned code more stable, with variation.
    - Wagner 2016: Type-3 clones "not a major source of faults in these systems".
  - Mondal 2018 attributes the disagreement to different tools, systems, setups and aspects of stability. Replicating the original studies with their original settings gave "mostly equivalent results".
  - Juergens (faulty/inconsistent groups, mean about 0.15) and Wagner (0.17) measure a similar ratio and agree numerically. Only the authors' interpretations differ.
- **Kapser & Godfrey's "71% beneficial" holds only for Apache at 30 tokens.**
  - The same paper reports 42% good / 39% harmful for Apache at 60 tokens, and 57% harmful for Gnumeric at 30 tokens.
  - The threshold effect runs in opposite directions in the two systems.
- **AI and duplication.**
  - GitClear reports rising duplication:
    - 2025: "4x" on the landing page, an "8-fold increase ... during 2024" and "approximately 10x" over two years in the report. Its table gives 0.45% → 1.80% → 6.66% of commits with a duplicate block.
    - 2026: block duplication +81% over 2023, and moved code down to 3.8%.
  - Against it:
    - Mao et al. 2026: human code was more duplicated (27.57% vs 14.29%).
    - Chen & Jiang 2024: many agents reduced duplication.
  - Ji et al. 2026 found substantial clones in LLM-detected code, but without a human baseline.
  - Every study attributes code to AI by proxy: adoption timing, comments mentioning tools, or detector output.
- **GitClear's 2025 appendix claims that Mondal and Wagner "agreed within 1.4%"**: 18.42% of buggy clones involved in bug propagation, against 17% of Type-3 groups containing faults. These measure different populations and outcomes, so the agreement is a vendor synthesis, not a replication.
- **Remove or keep?**
  - Remove:
    - Fowler/Beck: eliminating duplication "is a powerful way to drive out good designs"; adding it "is often papering over a problem".
    - PMD: "the most viable solution is to not duplicate".
    - dofm (HN 2026).
  - Keep:
    - Metz: "duplication is far cheaper than the wrong abstraction".
    - Abramov.
    - Dodds (AHA).
    - Google (DAMP tests).
    - Kim et al.: "aggressive, immediate refactoring may not be necessary or beneficial".
    - lokar (HN 2026).
  - Beck himself concedes that tests are where the two rules conflict.
- **Managing clones.**
  - PMD advises using CPD "to help remove duplicates, not to help keep duplicates in sync".
  - Wagner 2016 and Mondal 2014 recommend clone awareness, tool support and co-location for clones that cannot or should not be merged.
- **Cross-service code.**
  - For copying: the Go proverb, Russ Cox (both about *third-party* dependencies), and Kapser (independent external systems).
  - Against: SE.SE answer 337011 ("(almost) never correct" to duplicate the class for the same business entity), and hedora (HN 2026), who reports production data loss from duplicated flows.
- **Rule of three vs a change-history trigger.**
  - Wikipedia, citing Fowler's *Refactoring*, states the rule of three.
  - Dodds calls the related WET rule "dogmatic and over prescriptive".
  - Kim et al. suggest waiting, because many clones vanish on their own.
  - dofm argues that waiting lets the copies spread.

**Open questions**

- **What minimum clone size best separates harmful from harmless clones?**
  - Tool defaults disagree: jscpd 50 tokens / 5 lines; Sonar 100 tokens / 10 lines, or 10 Java statements; pylint 4 lines.
  - OMG ASCQM 1.1 uses a 90% function-similarity default, while its summary table cites 10% copy-pasted instructions per instance. Neither value is justified.
  - Kapser shows that the threshold changes harmful shares in ways that depend on the system.
- **Evidence for modern languages.** Most clone-fault studies cover Java, C, C# or COBOL from 2005–2018. There is little evidence for TypeScript, Python, Rust or Go, or for agent-maintained repositories. Kim et al.'s share of "unfactorable" clones predates modern generics and lambdas.
- **Do AI agents raise or lower the cost of duplication?**
  - They may miss siblings when fixing, according to practitioner reports.
  - They can also search for clones cheaply, e.g. via `jscpd --mcp`.
  - No longitudinal study of agent-maintained repositories was found.
- **Semantic (Type-4) clones are not covered by production detectors.** jscpd, CPD and Sonar are token-based. jscpd `--similarity` is AST-based but covers JS/TS only. How much duplicated knowledge hides there, and whether LLM-based clone detection is reliable enough for review, is unknown.
- **There is no validated metric for a "wrong abstraction".** Selector-parameter and boolean-trap rules are proxies. How many caller-specific branches make inverse refactoring worthwhile has not been studied.
- **GitClear's metrics are unvalidated.** Nobody has independently tested whether its copy/paste and duplicate-block metrics predict defects or maintenance cost. Its own multipliers are internally inconsistent.
- **Co-change as a merge trigger (F08)** is inferred from Kim et al. and expert advice. None of the sources here validates it against defect outcomes.
